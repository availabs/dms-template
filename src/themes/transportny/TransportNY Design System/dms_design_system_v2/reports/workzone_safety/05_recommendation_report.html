// recommendation for nysdot

Work Zone Safety & Mobility Reporting.

FHWA rewrote 23 CFR 630 Subpart J on November 1, 2024. By December 31, 2026 NYSDOT’s work-zone policy must name the safety and mobility performance measures it manages with; from 2025 it must monitor them every year; and by December 31, 2030 it must hand FHWA a data-driven programmatic review of a documented sample of its significant work zones. NYSDOT asked AVAIL to evaluate solutions and has suggested change in speed at work zones as a measure. This report defines that proposal, evaluates it against the rule, the literature and fifteen peer states, and recommends a program built on the data AVAIL and NYSDOT already hold.

23 CFR 630 Subpart J 89 FR 87293 · Nov 1 2024 NPMRDS × TRANSCOM compiled 2026-09-08 · draft for discussion
the proposal under evaluation
Report the change in vehicle speeds at work zones, from NPMRDS speeds and TRANSCOM work-zone events

NYSDOT has suggested meeting the rule’s mobility-measure requirement with a speed-based measure. Concretely, as we understand it:

measure

How much, and how often, speeds on the road segments inside a work zone fall while the zone is active, relative to speeds on the same segments when no work zone is present — a before / during comparison.

speed data

NPMRDS probe speeds and travel times — 5-minute, TMC segments, National Highway System — received free from FHWA and already held by AVAIL in npmrds2 (view 982, 2017 → present).

work-zone data (when / where)

TRANSCOM construction and maintenance events — the event store behind 511NY, held as source 956 / view 1947 — with the explicit option to substitute or combine better work-zone sources if any exist.

output

An annual (and, where useful, weekly) series by Region / facility / project that can be named in the Subpart J policy and used in the December 2030 programmatic review.

This report asks four things of that proposal: is it compliant with the rule as written; is it what peer states do; what does it leave uncovered (safety, exposure, project identity); and what data should be added, in what order.

// 01

Verdict on the proposal

Adopt it — it is not a proxy, it is one of the rule’s own example measures. §630.1006(b) requires the policy to identify performance measures and lists, verbatim, “percent of time when speeds in a work zone drop below a predefined threshold”; §630.1008(c) lists speeds as operational information and speed differentials as safety-surrogate data States shall use. FHWA has separately published guidance endorsing the National Performance Management Research Data Set for exactly this purpose [HOP-20-028, HOP-20-029], and the final-rule preamble confirms that “available” data means data accessible through existing sources — FHWA is not requiring States to buy anything [FR]. An NPMRDS × TRANSCOM speed-based mobility measure is therefore squarely compliant, costs nothing in data, and is roughly 80% built inside the TSMO work-zones dashboard pipeline.

Three refinements the literature insists on:

  1. Express it as an exceedance share, with a declared threshold and a pre-construction baseline — “% of active work-zone hours with in-zone speed below X”, not “average speed change”. Ohio uses 35 mph; FHWA’s TxDOT case uses travel time > 1.25 × baseline. Comparing against the posted limit alone is confounded by work-zone speed reductions [NCHRP 482].
  2. Pair it with the other rule-listed quantities that fall out of the same computation — delay (already computed), queue length and duration (contiguous upstream TMCs below threshold), and the speed differential between approach and zone, which doubles as the rule’s safety surrogate.
  3. It satisfies only half the clause. The policy also needs at least one safety measure; NYSDOT already produces work-zone crash and intrusion counts for the AWZSE legislative report [AWZSE 2026] — declare those.
the compliance floor
One mobility + one safety measure + a documented approach

FHWA’s preamble and Q&A: the listed measures are examples; States “can comply with this requirement by identifying at least one safety and one mobility performance measure” but “must have a documented work zone performance management approach for selecting projects, identifying performance measures, and collecting performance data.”

What the 2024 rule actually requires

The 2004 rule asked States to consider impacts and review their processes every two years. The 2024 rewrite turns that into a measurement program with a calendar. Full clause-by-clause read: 01_regulation_23cfr630J.md.

ClauseRequirement (2024 text)Consequence for NYSDOT
§630.1006(b) “At a minimum, the policy shall identify safety and mobility performance measures that will be used to manage work zone performance.” Examples: fatal/injury WZ crashes; % projects exceeding a pre-established crash rate; worker fatalities/injuries; % projects with queues above a threshold; % of time speeds in a WZ drop below a threshold. HDM Chapter 16 — NYSDOT’s policy vehicle — contains no quantitative measures today [HDM 16]. Must be amended by Dec 31 2026.
§630.1008(c) States shall use field observations, available crash data, available safety surrogate data (speed differentials, hard braking, CAV data), available operational information (speeds, travel times, queue length, duration) and available exposure data (number of projects, number/length of lane closures, VMT through work zones) — for specific projects during implementation and for the programmatic review. Four data families must be demonstrably in use. Preamble: “available” = accessible through existing sources; no purchase obligation.
§630.1008(e) Programmatic review every 5 years: data-driven assessment of a documented representative sample of significant work zones; performance since last review; processes to improve; action items, responsible offices, timeline; annual monitoring; cross-office scope incl. permitting. A standing annual series, not a one-off study. Next report Dec 31 2030 covering CY 2025–2029.
§630.1010(c) Interstate projects within a TMA requiring intermittent or continuous lane closures for 3 or more consecutive days are significant (IIJA §11303(a)). The sample frame. NY TMAs: New York–Newark, Buffalo, Rochester, Albany, Syracuse, Poughkeepsie–Newburgh (HDM §16.5.2.1, App. 16B).
§630.1004 Mobility: “minimum delay compared to baseline travel when no work zone is present”; measures: delay, speed, travel time, queue length. Work zone crash: MMUCC 5th ed., incl. vehicles slowed/stopped by the zone before the first sign. Every mobility measure is a before/during comparison; the crash definition includes queue-related crashes upstream.
§630.1016 Comply no later than December 31, 2026; next programmatic review due December 31, 2030. The calendar.

Compliance on data we hold
  1. The data, graded against the rule’s four families

Full inventory with audit numbers: 04_datasets.md. Three findings from the audit changed the recommendation.

finding 1 · indicator data
The 511NY WZDx feed is TRANSCOM — and it is thin

Snapshot 2026-09-08: 6,232 active features, publisher Arcadis, data_source_id: TRANSCOM — the same store AVAIL holds as source 956/1947. Geometry is MultiPoint (start/end only); direction, vehicle_impact, location_method are “unknown” on 100%; lane records on 0; all start dates unverified; schedules are free text. Our internal copy (lanes affected, direction, tmclist) is richer than the public feed.

finding 3 · project identity
STIP gives PINs, not geometry; NYSDOT’s project map is token-gated

The STIP list is one row per PIN × phase × fund year with Region, MPO, county and description — the project universe for the review sample — but no location or closure dates. NYSDOT’s ActiveProjects MapServer (behind “Projects in Your Neighborhood”) returns 499 Token Required. TRANSCOM events are closures, not projects. The PIN↔event link is the one data request that makes the 2030 sample deterministic.

the literature, in one paragraph

FHWA’s measurement canon (2011–2021) settled the taxonomy — exposure, safety, mobility — and the finding that probe speeds are adequate for programmatic mobility reporting while the binding constraint is knowing when and where the work zone actually was [HOP-13-011, HOP-13-043]. No NCHRP product targets the 2024 rule’s measurement directly; the closest are FHWA’s Iowa and Illinois data-driven review case studies and the SWZDI pooled fund’s 2025 WZPERFOMAT tool, which implements exactly the “work-zone feed × probe speeds × crash records” architecture AVAIL already operates [SWZDI 2025]. Connected-vehicle hard braking is the gold-standard safety surrogate but is paid data; the only peer-reviewed paper written to the rule is Purdue’s (Jan 2026), now applied to 101 work zones in 9 states [Sakhare 2026]. Full review: 02_literature_review.md.

Limitations to state up front — and how we handle them

LimitationHandling
TRANSCOM is closures, not projects, and includes permit/utility workDe-duplicate recurring chains; classify by nysdot_sub_category; reconcile to PINs for the review sample; report utility/permit work separately (a permitting finding the review must make anyway).
Work-zone extents: tmclist on ~50% of events; others only start/end pointsSnap points to the TMC network along the facility; flag extent confidence; expect measured impact to grow as extents improve (Illinois observed this).
TMC resolutionProgrammatic reporting is fine; project-level queue lengths are estimates (every source). Report queue as range / 95th pct; use XD or devices for a few flagship projects if enforcement-grade numbers are wanted.
NHS-only speedsOff-NHS work zones get exposure and crash measures but no speed measures. State the coverage; it matches every peer using NPMRDS/INRIX TMC.
Crash location: open data has no coordinates (56% ref-marker)Tier 2 CLEAR extract; until then statewide/county series plus a ref-marker-geocoded subset.
Latency: NPMRDS ~2 weeks; event×TMC delay is batchAnnual/monthly reporting, not real-time; weekly snapshots feasible from raw NPMRDS.
Baseline contamination by prior zones, incidents, weatherExclude epochs overlapping other TRANSCOM events; use 12-month same-hour/day-type medians (HOP-20-029).

  1. Which office owns the Subpart J policy update (HDM Ch. 16 vs a standalone policy), and who is FHWA NY Division’s counterpart?
