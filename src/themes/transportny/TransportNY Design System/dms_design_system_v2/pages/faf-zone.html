// 01executive summary

Why the zone is the level that matters.

FAF is built on the Commodity Flow Survey, and the survey's sampling frame is the FAF zone list — the county-level technical report puts it plainly: “FAF zones and CFS zones are equivalent.” That has a hard consequence. Aggregations of zones are statistically valid; subdivisions of them are not estimates at all. New York's five zones are wildly unequal: zone 363, the New York portion of the New York–Newark combined statistical area, receives 306.6M tons against Rochester's 46.5M, and by itself accounts for 47.7% of state tonnage on an involved basis. Zone 369, Remainder of New York, is the opposite problem: forty-eight counties from Plattsburgh to Jamestown treated as one origin and one destination. Both zones are the reason a corridor question cannot be answered from this file alone.

key findings
  • Zone 363 receives 2.4× what it ships, by weight
  • Rochester and Rest of NY are the only zones that ship more than they receive
  • The Rest of NY → New York part lane runs 5:1 against the reverse
  • Zone 363 is a CSA and stops at the state line — its NJ, CT and PA halves are zones 341, 092, 423
  • The Port of New York and New Jersey is in zone 341, which is New Jersey

Two entries are the tell of a consumption centre: waste and scrap at #4 (24.5M tons, an export of the city’s own metabolism) and mixed freight at #6 (22.1M tons but $70.3B, a third of the state’s mixed-freight value).

Zone 363 is not New York City, and calling it that will mis-read the data.

Its full name is New York–Newark, NY-NJ-CT-PA CFS Area (NY Part). It is a combined statistical area, so on the New York side it reaches up the Hudson to Dutchess County and out to Suffolk — far beyond the five boroughs. And it stops at the state line, because FAF zones never cross one: the New Jersey, Connecticut and Pennsylvania portions of the same metro area are separate zones 341, 092 and 423. In FAF6 code 423 is reassigned entirely, to Allentown–Bethlehem–Easton, so a join on zone code across versions will silently produce wrong answers.

How the zone codes are built

The first two digits are the state FIPS code; the third distinguishes sub-state zones, with 9 reserved for “Remainder of” and 0 for a whole state. So 36 is New York, 361–364 are its metro zones and 369 is the remainder; 020 is Alaska, entire. This pattern holds across all 132 codes but no FAF document states it as a rule, so treat it as an observation, not a contract. The published crosswalk pairs each zone with a CFS area code — 363 is 36-408, 369 is 36-99999.

Rest of New York is forty-eight counties

Zone 369 is a single origin and a single destination covering everything outside the four metro CSAs — the North Country, the Southern Tier, the Finger Lakes outside Rochester. It ships 152.6M tons and receives 128.4M, and the file offers no way to tell whether a ton of it left Plattsburgh or Jamestown. That is not a defect of the release; it is the sample design. Anything finer is a disaggregation, with its own error.

// 05the construction rule and what it permits

A zone exists because the survey can sample it.

FAF5 User’s Guide §2.1.1, verbatim
Metropolitan areas consist of Metropolitan Statistical Areas or Consolidated Statistical Areas as defined by the Office of Management and Budget. When a metropolitan area is entirely within a state or when a state’s portion of a multi-state metropolitan area is large enough to support the sampling procedures in the CFS, the area becomes a separate FAF region. Small single-state metropolitan areas and small portions of a multi-state metropolitan area are part of the State or Remainder of State.

That sentence is the whole geography. A zone exists because the survey can sample it, not because it is a meaningful freight market — which is why Rest of New York exists at all, and why the four New York metro zones are combined statistical areas rather than the metro areas a planner would draw. The 2017 survey kept the count at 132 and moved exactly three counties between zones: Tallapoosa County into Birmingham, Fannin County into Dallas–Fort Worth, and Jefferson Davis Parish into Lake Charles–Jennings.
