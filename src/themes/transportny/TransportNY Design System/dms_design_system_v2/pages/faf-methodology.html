Three asymmetries that break naive code.

There is no current_value_2017 and none for any forecast year — the nominal series exists for 2018–2024 only, so it cannot be extended in either direction. There is no 2025 column, or 2026 through 2029: the file jumps from 2024 to 2030. And this release has no scenario columns: the dictionary documents tons_high, tons_low, value_high and value_low for 2025–2050, and FAF5.7.1 does not contain them. Its forecast columns are baseline only.

Two consequences worth carrying

The survey's international coverage was discarded. “Since the CFS undercounts U.S. export and does not account for U.S. imports, only domestic shipments from the CFS were used for constructing FAF5 Origin-Destination commodity flow data” — the foreign trade rows come from a separate build. And two supplements have essentially no value: municipal solid waste is 381 million tons at zero recorded value, construction and demolition debris 569 million tons at $4 billion. Any value-weighted screen writes them out.

The eleven supplements

Farm-based agriculture · municipal solid waste · construction and demolition debris · retail · services · household and business moves · crude petroleum · natural gas · foreign trade · fisheries · logging. Four dominate: natural gas, foreign trade, farm-based shipments and crude petroleum, on both weight and value. Natural gas alone is 10.2% of national tonnage, which is why energy commodities top every tonnage ranking in this family.

the survey covers mining, manufacturing, wholesale, retail and services plus some auxiliary establishments such as warehouses

Suppression and imputation.

Over 90% of domestic movements are captured in unsuppressed cells, and not all of them are released — for shipper confidentiality, for sampling variability above a 50% coefficient of variation, and for activity too infrequent to sample. The important number is the last row.

So 40% of the cells are imputed, and 8% of the tonnage

The full origin-destination-commodity-mode matrix is 40.2% suppressed by cell count, which is 7.7% of tonnage and 6.2% of value. The gaps were filled in two steps: a seed matrix built from the survey's public-use micro-data, which covers 97% of the suppressed cells, with the remaining 3% estimated by sequentially applied maximum likelihood — a change from FAF4's log-linear method — then iterative proportional fitting against every known marginal total, then quality checks on value-to-weight ratios, iterated. The practical reading: marginal totals are strong and individual deep cells are weak, which is the same conclusion the zone-size caveat reaches from the other direction.

this is also why an origin-destination-commodity-mode query on one small zone pair should be treated as an estimate of an estimate

The construction rule, verbatim

“Metropolitan areas consist of Metropolitan Statistical Areas or Consolidated Statistical Areas as defined by the Office of Management and Budget. When a metropolitan area is entirely within a state or when a state's portion of a multi-state metropolitan area is large enough to support the sampling procedures in the CFS, the area becomes a separate FAF region. Small single-state metropolitan areas and small portions of a multi-state metropolitan area are part of the State or Remainder of State.” Zone types: C combined statistical area, M metropolitan statistical area, R rest of state, S a state with no CSA or MSA, SM a whole state inside one MSA (only DC and Rhode Island).

FAF5 User's Guide §2.1.1 and Table 3 · the 2017 survey kept the count at 132 and moved three counties between zones

Versions, and FAF6's breaking changes.

FAF has been rebuilt on each Commodity Flow Survey since 2002. FAF1 was different: it covered only truck, rail and water tonnage for 1998.

GenerationBenchmark surveyNotes
FAF1—truck, rail and water tonnage for calendar year 1998 only
FAF22002 CFS—
FAF32007 CFS—
FAF42012 CFSvalue in 2012 constant dollars
FAF52017 CFSreleased as FAF5.0 in February 2021 · value in 2017 constant dollars · this site uses FAF5.7.1
FAF62022 CFS (inferred)user guide dated July 2026 · value in 2022 dollars · 134 zones · ton-miles “planned for later release”
update cadence is every five years with the economic census · that FAF6 is benchmarked on the 2022 survey is an inference from the dollar base and the historical series, not a sentence in the guide
Zone code 423 means two different places in FAF5 and FAF6.

In FAF5, 423 is the Pennsylvania part of the New York–Newark CFS area — and it is New York's fifth-largest outbound partner by tonnage. In FAF6 the New York–Newark area drops its Pennsylvania portion and code 423 is reassigned to Allentown–Bethlehem–Easton, PA-NJ. A join on zone code across the two versions will produce wrong answers with no error and no warning. The other FAF6 changes: Buffalo's zone is renamed Buffalo-Cheektowaga-Olean; Iowa splits from one zone 190 into 191 Des Moines and 199 Remainder; and 395 Toledo-Findlay-Tiffin is added — taking 132 to 134.

What this page will not do: reconcile FAF against TRANSEARCH.

The Freight Atlas headlines 936.5 million tons for New York in 2021 from S&P Global TRANSEARCH; FAF gives 729.8 million for the same year. Through movements explain most of the gap — the 2024 plan puts them at about 38% of state tonnage, and FAF's regional database has no through concept at all. But not all of it: the plan reports 159.3 million tons of water freight against FAF's 7.9 million, which is a difference of kind rather than of coverage and implies a different underlying dataset. So the two totals are named as different universes on both the home and state pages, and no crosswalk is asserted. Never mix the two in one chart.
