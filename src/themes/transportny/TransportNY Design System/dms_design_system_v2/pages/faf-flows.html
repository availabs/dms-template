// 01executive summary

One neighbour dominates, and the second-biggest flow is not New York's at all.

New York's largest trading partner in every cut of the data is FAF zone 341, the New Jersey portion of the same metropolitan area: 48.0 million tons inbound (22.4% of all inbound tonnage) and 18.9 million outbound. Add zones 092 and 423 — the Connecticut and Pennsylvania portions — and the single metro area New York shares with three other states accounts for 28.1% of inbound and 25.7% of outbound tonnage. Beyond it the lanes are Pennsylvania and New England by weight, and California and the Sun Belt by value. Internationally, Canada is 57% of the tonnage New York actually receives and Europe is 29% of its value. And 76,983 thousand tons of the freight in New York's rows is not New York's freight at all: it entered or left through a New York gateway on its way between two other places.

key findings
  • The New York–Newark metro area’s other three state parts are 28.1% of inbound tons
  • Los Angeles is 15th outbound by tons and 3rd by value
  • Montana is 8th inbound by tons — pipeline energy, not manufactured freight
  • Gateway flows are overwhelmingly Canadian: 31.1M t in, 35.4M t out
  • Export tonnage grows fastest of the three trade types, at 2.3% a year

The metro area is four zones and three of them are other states

FAF zones never cross a state line, so the New York–Newark CFS area is split into 363 (NY), 341 (NJ), 092 (CT) and 423 (PA). Freight moving between Manhattan and Newark is therefore an interstate flow in this dataset, and the three sibling zones together are 28.1% of New York's inbound tonnage. Any regional analysis of the metro area has to reassemble it from four rows — and in FAF6, code 423 is reassigned to Allentown, so the reassembly changes.

Why the two rankings disagree

Ranked by weight, the lanes are a neighbours list — New Jersey, Pennsylvania, New England — because heavy freight is short-haul freight. Ranked by value, Los Angeles is third in both directions on about a tenth of the tonnage, and San Francisco, Dallas, Miami and Atlanta appear. Those are container and air lanes carrying electronics, pharmaceuticals and apparel. Publishing one ranking answers half the question.

What is in each of the eight regions

The groupings follow United Nations Statistics Division definitions, and three of them will surprise you. Puerto Rico and the US Virgin Islands are in 803, Rest of Americas — not domestic. Turkey is in 806, South-West and Central Asia, not Europe. Russia and Ukraine are in 804, Europe. Region 807, Eastern Asia, is China, Hong Kong, Japan, Macao, Mongolia, North Korea, South Korea and Taiwan. Full country lists are in Appendix A of the user guide.

FAF5 User's Guide §1.3 and Appendix A · ORNL/TM-2021/2154 §9.1
“Zone of entry” is a whole region, not a port — and the term replaced “international gateway” deliberately.

The user guide is explicit: “Zone of Entry — This is the FAF region or state where an import enters the United States. This term refers to the entire region or state and does not limit to any single location. This replaces the term ‘international gateway’ used previously.” So there is no bridge, no crossing, no marine terminal anywhere in the regional database. Import and export modes are also partly assumed: imports from Canada and Mexico were “generally assumed to have the same foreign and domestic mode unless that assumption produced an unfeasible result.”

The Port of New York and New Jersey is FAF zone 341, and zone 341 is New Jersey.

FHWA's forecast report names it outright — “Imports at FAF zone 341 (the Port of New York and New Jersey)” — and ranks it the fifth-largest US import gateway at 63 million tons in 2017 rising to 96 million by 2050. The reason is mechanical: the harbour is one customs district, FAF zones do not cross state lines, and the convention (documented for FAF4 and carried forward) assigns the whole district to the New Jersey zone that contains part of the port. FHWA also notes the mix shift: predominantly gasoline and fuel oils in 2017, with nonmetallic minerals from Africa and the Americas the second-largest commodity by 2050, growing at 4.1% a year. None of this appears in a query filtered to New York zones.

And no crossing-level detail at all

New York shares 445 miles of border with Ontario and Quebec and has sixteen land ports of entry for trucks. FAF knows none of them: its Canadian tonnage arrives in a zone, not at a bridge. The 2024 State Freight Plan carries the per-crossing counts from a different source — the Peace Bridge at over 885,000 US-bound trucks in 2022, Lewiston–Queenston over 375,000, Champlain about 280,000, Alexandria Bay about 200,000 — and those are counts of vehicles, not FAF tonnage. The two cannot be joined at the facility.
