// 01executive summary

What FAF says about New York freight.

In the 2017 benchmark year, 754.9 million tons of freight worth $1.358 trillion (constant 2017 dollars) moved to, from or within New York's five FAF zones. More of it stayed inside the state than crossed a line in either direction: 401.4M tons, 53.2%, had both endpoints in New York, and two thirds of that moved under 100 miles. Trucks carried 73.5% of the tonnage and 74.8% of the value; pipeline was the second-largest mode by weight at 17.3% and almost invisible by value at 1.9%. The single largest trading partner in both directions and on both measures is the New Jersey part of the New York–Newark CFS area — FAF zone 341, which is also where FAF puts the entire Port of New York and New Jersey. By 2050 the baseline forecast has New York tonnage up 58.4% and value up 96.5%, a 1.4% and 2.1% compound annual rate; exports grow fastest at 2.3% a year.

key findings
  • 53.2% of NY freight never leaves the state, and 66.5% of that moves under 100 miles
  • 77.0M tons is gateway-only — neither origin nor destination is in New York
  • New York receives 33.0M tons of imports but is the entry point for 66.2M
  • Pipeline is 17.3% of tons and 1.9% of value; air is 0.07% of tons and 4.1% of value
  • Canada is 75% of import tonnage; Europe is the largest source by value

// 03the decomposition the workbooks omit

What New York sends and receives, versus what merely passes through it.

Seven disjoint classes, one identity. Every NY-involved row is a domestic flow with one or both ends in New York, an import whose destination or merely whose entry point is here, or an export whose origin or merely whose exit point is here. Sort them properly and receipts + shipments − within-state + gateway reproduces the involved total to the unit — which is how you know the sort is right.

“New York imports” on the standard basis is double what New York actually imports.

The data dictionary is explicit: dms_orig is “the US entry region where an import enters United States” and dms_dest is “the US exit region where an export leaves.” So on the standard total-flows basis a New York row can be an import that merely landed here, or an export that merely left from here. Filtering trade_type = 2 on a New York zone gives 66,210 thousand tons; filtering it on New York as the destination gives 32,956. Both are defensible numbers with completely different meanings, and only one of them belongs under a heading that says what New York consumes.

The Port of New York and New Jersey is a New Jersey zone

FHWA's forecast report names FAF zone 341 — the New Jersey part of the New York–Newark CFS area — as “the Port of New York and New Jersey,” and ranks it the 5th largest US import gateway at 63M tons in 2017 rising to 96M by 2050. The convention, documented for FAF4 and carried forward, puts the whole customs district in 341 because FAF zones do not cross state lines. Zone 341 is separately New York's number-one trading partner in both directions on both measures. A harbour analysis built only on New York zones will understate it badly.

Do not compare this rail share to FAF4's.

The 2017 Commodity Flow Survey changed how it decides a rail or water transfer point, adding commodity to the volume-and-distance test. Published single-mode rail tonnage fell 16 percentage points and rail value fell 26, with the difference moving into truck-rail — that is, into multiple modes & mail. FHWA's own base-year report says the change “did cause comparability issues with prior FAF data” and that a separate synchronised historical series exists for trend work. Rail did not lose a quarter of its value between 2012 and 2017; the definition moved.

Two commodity groups carry almost no value at all

In the national benchmark composition, municipal solid waste is recorded with zero value and construction & demolition debris with $4 billion against 569 million tons. They are real freight with real network impact and no price. Any value-weighted analysis — a benefit-cost ratio, an economic-impact multiplier, a value-per-mile screen — systematically writes them out.

Canada by weight, Europe by value

Canada is 57% of the international tonnage New York actually receives and 52% of what it sends, but only 16% of import value. Europe is 11% of import tonnage and 29% of import value. The eight FAF foreign regions are defined off United Nations Statistics Division groupings; note that Puerto Rico and the US Virgin Islands sit in region 803, Rest of Americas, and Turkey sits in 806 rather than 804.

foreign trade legs are truncated at the border, or at the first/last US airport for air
The workbooks stop at five partners; this stops at eight

FAF's published state workbooks give “top five trade partners” and a top-50 origin–destination table. Neither is a ranking of all 131 partner zones, which is what a corridor question actually needs. The trade-lane page carries the full ranking both ways on both measures.

The distance is a weighted average, and only the domestic leg

dist_band is documented as “the average weighted distance of shipments” for “the US domestic portion only.” International legs are cut at the border or the coast — or, for air, at the first or last US airport. So a container from Shanghai to Syracuse is banded on its US mileage, not its voyage, and the same truncation governs every ton-mile figure on this site.

The estimated years are flat to slightly down — 2024 is 3.6% below the 2017 benchmark — and then the forecast steps up 21.9% by 2030. The discontinuity is a methodology boundary, not an event: annual estimates and forecasts are produced by different processes, and the forecast’s macro inputs were frozen at April 2021. There is no 2025 column to bridge them.

FAF has no through movements, and this is structural.

Every row in the regional database is an origin–destination pair, so freight that crosses New York without stopping is in no New York total on this page. The 2024 State Freight Plan, working from TRANSEARCH, puts through movements at about 38% of state tonnage and 42% of state value — 355.4M tons in 2021. If your question is about pavement, bridges, truck parking or corridor capacity, that is the largest single category and FAF cannot see it. The gateway row in § 03 is not a substitute: it counts freight that touched a New York port, not freight that drove across the state.

And no confidence intervals, anywhere.

FAF publishes point estimates only. The county-level technical report explains why in the course of describing its own validation: “Validation of the benchmark and annual estimates among FAF regions defies traditional methods of calculating estimation variability since multiple data sources are blended with techniques that can either offset or magnify sampling errors in the source data.” Read the digits as magnitudes. In the source survey, 40.2% of full origin-destination-commodity-mode cells are suppressed and imputed — 7.7% of tonnage and 6.2% of value.

Reproducing this page

Filter FAF5.7.1.csv to rows where dms_orig or dms_dest is in {361, 362, 363, 364, 369}, then group. Direction is derived: both endpoints New York is within, destination only is inbound, origin only is outbound. The three subtotals sum to the NY-involved total exactly; receipts and shipments do not, because within-state flows are in both.
