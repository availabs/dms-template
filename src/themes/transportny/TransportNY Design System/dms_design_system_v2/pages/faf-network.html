// 01executive summary

The one place FAF knows where freight goes.

FAF's model network has 487,384 links and 348,498 nodes, dualised centrelines on every divided highway, every key interchange ramp, and centroid connectors to all US counties, major cargo airports, water ports and border crossings. The 2017 assignment puts 5,943,495 daily truck-VMT on New York's 13,147 flow-carrying links — 2.2% of the national total — and 45.7 billion annual truck ton-miles. That second figure is worth pausing on: the regional database says New York-involved truck movements generate 117.7 billion ton-miles anywhere in the country, while this says 45.7 billion ton-miles occur on New York roads. They are different quantities, and the assigned one is the only FAF number that includes freight crossing the state with no New York endpoint. Two thirds of the VMT is on Interstates and, against intuition for a state with New York City in it, 57.7% of it is rural.

key findings
  • 64.2% of New York's assigned truck-VMT is on Interstates
  • 57.7% is rural, on 10,152 of the 15,277 flow-carrying centerline miles
  • Combination units are 53.1% of VMT and 88.6% of tonnage
  • The median flow-carrying link sees 124 trucks a day; the 99th percentile sees 2,666
  • The two highest New York values are model artifacts, not roads

Do not sum a link tons column into a state total.

One shipment traverses many links, so a tons column summed over links counts that shipment once per link it touches. New York's 13,147 flow-carrying links hold a tons total that means nothing. The correct aggregates are VMT and ton-miles — flow multiplied by link length — and those are what every figure on this page is built from. It is the same trap as summing a traffic count over segments.

The flow tables have no geography whatsoever

Six CSVs, 79 columns, and the only identifying field is ID. No state, no county, no route number. Every place name on this page came from joining ID to the network's own geometry, where STATE, CTFIPS, FAFZONE, F_Class and NHFN live. And ID is not row order: it is a sparse TransCAD internal identifier running to 1,949,504 across 487,394 links. All 316,070 flow rows matched a link in all six tables, with zero unmatched.

Through traffic finally appears

The state page names FAF's missing through movements as a hard structural gap, and this is the partial answer. The assignment routes all US origin–destination pairs over the network, so a New York link carries freight from every lane whose shortest path crosses the state — Ohio to Massachusetts included. That is why 45.7 billion assigned ton-miles on New York links is not the same quantity as 117.7 billion truck ton-miles of New York-involved flows, and why the assignment is the right product for a pavement or corridor question even though the regional database is right for an economic one.

The highest New York value in the file is not a road carrying 12,400 trucks a day.

NY 304 in Rockland County at 12,400, and SMITH ST at 7,576, sit immediately beside a zone centroid loading point — the node where the model injects all of zone 363's freight onto the network. Everything the zone generates funnels across those few links before dispersing, so they carry the whole zone's loading rather than a plausible street volume. NY 304 is a principal arterial not on the freight network at all; a real 12,400-truck day would make it the busiest freight facility in the state by a factor of two. The credible top of the distribution is the George Washington Bridge at 5,388 and the Tappan Zee at 5,346. Eighty-six links are explicit centroid connectors holding 1.95% of New York's assigned VMT, and those are easy to drop; these four are ordinary road links next door to one, and they are not.

FHWA's own published maps are thresholded, and the tables are not

The national truck-flow maps in FHWA's material show “domestic and international freight moving by truck on highway segments with more than 25 FAF trucks per day and between places typically more than fifty miles apart.” The flow tables themselves carry every link, including the long tail below that threshold and the artifacts above it. Reproducing an FHWA map from the tables without applying the same two filters produces a different picture and an argument about whose is wrong.

This is not truck AADT and it must not be compared to HPMS.

FAF assigns the freight trucks in its own origin–destination table onto its own network: about 96.6 billion annual US truck-VMT, which is roughly a third of the HPMS total truck VMT. The missing two thirds is local delivery, service and construction traffic, empty repositioning and everything on roads the network does not represent. So a FAF link value next to a state count station will always be lower, and the gap is not an error in either. For real observed truck volumes on New York's system, use the state's own counts or NPMRDS.
