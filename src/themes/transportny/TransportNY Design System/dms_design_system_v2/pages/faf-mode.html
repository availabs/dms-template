// 01executive summary

Every mode's rank depends on which measure you pick.

Trucks carry 73.5% of New York's freight tonnage and 74.8% of its value, and on either measure the ordering below them is different. Pipeline is second by weight at 17.3% and seventh by value at 1.9%. Multiple modes & mail — containerised and parcel freight — is fourth by weight at 3.3% and second by value at 16.9%. Air is 0.07% of tons and 4.1% of value. The measure that reconciles them is average haul: truck 212 miles, pipeline 278, rail 715, water 899, air 1,212, multiple modes 1,229. Short, cheap, heavy freight moves by truck and pipe; long, dear, light freight moves by air and container. And one trap dominates the whole page: FAF5's rail figures cannot be compared to FAF4's, because the 2017 survey moved much single-mode rail into multiple modes and mail.

key findings
  • Truck's share is 87.8% of within-state tons but only 54.0% of inbound
  • Pipeline is 30.6% of inbound tonnage — the down-state fuel and gas load
  • Mode 8 has zero New York tonnage, and there is a reason
  • Rail fell 16 points of tonnage share between the 2012 and 2017 surveys by definition
  • One combination-unit truck carries 16.11 tons on the published factors

Mode 8 is zero here, and that is a fact rather than a gap

“No domestic mode” exists for one situation only: imported crude petroleum transferred directly from an inbound ship to a US refinery at the zone of entry. New York has no such refinery, so the row is a true zero. Nationally it is 208.6 million tons — 1.1% of US tonnage — almost all of it on the Gulf coast. The category exists so those flows are counted without being attributed to a domestic network they never touch.

Truck’s 212 miles is the shortest, and it carries three quarters of the freight — the distance profile and the modal profile are the same fact seen twice. Multiple modes & mail at 1,229 miles is longer than air, which looks wrong and is not.

Multiple modes & mail has a longer average haul than air. That is real.

Two reasons, both in the definitions. First, air's domestic ground legs are booked to truck: “in the case of imports and exports by air, domestic moves by ground to and from the port of entry or exit are categorized with Truck,” so air's ton-miles are the flown segment only. Second, the ton-mile field counts the US domestic leg only and truncates international legs at the border, the coast, or the first or last US airport — which shortens air disproportionately, because air is where the international share is highest. Multiple modes & mail, by contrast, is dominated by long domestic container and parcel movements that are measured end to end.

Four mode fields, and three of them get ignored

dms_mode is the domestic mode. fr_inmode is the mode between the foreign region and the US entry point on an import; fr_outmode is the mode between the US exit point and the foreign region on an export. Both are blank on domestic rows and both take values 1–7, never 8. An analysis that reads only dms_mode has silently dropped every ocean and international air leg in the file — which is fine if the question is about domestic infrastructure, and wrong if it is about supply chains.

This is the single most likely way to publish a false finding from FAF.

The 2017 Commodity Flow Survey started using commodity, as well as volume and distance, to decide where a rail or water transfer point was. In ORNL's words, “many previously would be classified as single mode of rail/water shipments were assigned to truck-rail/truck-water (i.e. multiple mode) shipments under the 2017 CFS.” The report is explicit about the consequence: the change “did cause comparability issues with prior FAF data. Therefore, adjustments were needed on historical FAF databases to allow for a consistent comparison over time.” A rail trend chart drawn straight from FAF4 and FAF5 shows a collapse that did not happen. Use the synchronised historical series.

The crosswalk that produced these eight

The survey's own mode codes are collapsed into FAF's eight: CFS 04 and 05 become truck; 06 becomes rail; 08, 09, 10 and 101 become water; 11 becomes air; 12 becomes pipeline; 19 becomes other and unknown; and 14 through 18 all become multiple modes & mail. That last line is where the rail break lands. FAF and the survey deliberately say multiple modes and mail rather than intermodal, because the category runs from a container to coal moving mine-to-railhead by truck — and because a shipper using a parcel service usually does not know what modes were involved after pickup.

Converting FAF tons into truck counts is a separate, weaker step.

FAF publishes tonnage; a corridor study needs vehicles. The published payload factors above make the arithmetic easy and the provenance is the problem: the report they come from is titled “Proposed freight analysis framework version 4 payload factors,” it is a 2020 recommendation rather than a statement of what FAF5 did, and it rests on 2002 vehicle-survey micro-data. The report warns about its own per-commodity tables — the single-unit/combination-unit split has a standard error of 11%, and it singles out animal feed, coal and fertiliser as cases where “caution is suggested.” Waste and scrap is the only commodity that is majority single-unit, at 51.6%. If you need truck counts on a link, the assignment flow tables already carry them and did this conversion for you.

What each of the eight actually includes.

The exclusions matter more than the inclusions. Four of the eight definitions exist mainly to say what they are not, and three of those four hand the excluded freight to multiple modes & mail.

1
Truck

Private and for-hire trucks. Does not include truck that is part of multiple modes & mail, or truck moving in conjunction with domestic air cargo.

6
Pipeline

Crude petroleum, natural gas and product pipelines. Also includes pipeline from offshore wells to land, which the Army Corps counts as a water move.

2
Rail

Any common carrier or private railroad. Does not include rail that is part of multiple modes & mail.

5
Multiple modes & mail

Multiple modes and parcel delivery, US Postal Service or couriers, capped at 150 pounds. Not limited to containerised or trailer-on-flatcar shipments.

3
Water

Shallow draft, deep draft, Great Lakes and intra-port shipments. Does not include water that is part of multiple modes & mail.

7
Other and unknown

Movements not elsewhere classified, such as flyaway aircraft, and shipments whose mode cannot be determined.

4
Air (incl. truck-air)

Shipments by air or by a combination of truck and air. For imports and exports by air, the domestic ground move to and from the port of entry or exit is categorised as truck.

8
No domestic mode

Shipments with an international mode and no domestic mode — limited to imported crude petroleum transferred directly from inbound ships to a US refinery at the zone of entry. This exists so flows that use no domestic network are accounted for properly.
