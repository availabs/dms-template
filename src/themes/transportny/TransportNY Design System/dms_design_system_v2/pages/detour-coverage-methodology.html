How coverage points get picked.

When an author asks "what happens if this road closes," the plugin tests up to 100 realistic origin/destination pairs around the closure. This page walks through, in order, how it decides which points on each side are worth testing.

Points per side up to 10 Search radius 8 mi Target spacing 1 mi

The rules, in order

Each rule runs on the result of the one before it — this is a pipeline, not six independent settings.

# Rule What it does
1 Seed Start from the first real junction, not the segment's own end. OSM splits roads at every intersection, so the closed segment's own endpoint is usually already sitting at one — not a place a real traveler would start or end a trip. From each end, the plugin walks outward along the straightest continuation of the road until it reaches a node that actually offers more than one way forward. That's the seed for that side.
2 Flood Search outward from each seed, nearest point first. Every reachable road out to 8 miles is collected and sorted nearest-to-farthest, each tagged same road (matches the closure's own road type) or other (branched onto a different one). The two sides are kept from crossing into each other's territory.
3 Trust The single nearest point is accepted automatically. A point right next to the closure is almost always a genuine, direct use of it — taken on trust rather than spending a validation check on it.
4 Validate Every other candidate has to prove it's real. For each remaining candidate (nearest first, up to 300 tried per side), the plugin checks: does the open-road route from this point actually pass through the closed segment? If not, it's discarded.
5 Prefer Stay on the same road whenever it can supply enough points. If ten well-spaced, validated points exist on the closure's own road, that's the full result. Branching onto a different road only fills the count when the same road genuinely can't.
6 Select Ten points, spaced at least a mile apart — when the road allows it. Points are picked nearest-to-farthest, skipping anything closer than a mile to the last pick. Reaching all ten always wins: if a short or dense local road can't fit ten points a full mile apart, the spacing shrinks instead of returning fewer points.
Known trade-off

On a short or tightly-gridded local street network, this can mean two points land closer than a mile apart — the count of ten is currently the requirement, the 1 mile spacing is what gives way. This is current, documented behavior, not a bug awaiting a fix.

Worked example

An illustrative closure on a long, mostly-straight road — ten points land on the same road, spaced close to a mile apart.

Start side · seed to farthest pick 7 mi shown
Closed segment Seed · first real junction Candidate · same road Candidate · other road
  1. Walk from the closure's own end to the first junction with a real choice of direction — the seed, about 0.3 mi out.
  2. Flood out to 8 mi, tag every point same-road or other, validate nearest-first.
  3. The same road alone supplies more than ten validated points — no need to branch onto a side street.
  4. Walking nearest to farthest, skipping anything under a mile from the last pick, lands exactly ten points between the seed and about 7.4 mi out.

Source: selectClosureDensityCandidates in data-types/routing/memoryGraph.js. The same/other-road tagging and the 1-mile target reflect the plugin's current, settled behavior.
