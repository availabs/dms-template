Incident delay and clearance.

Two measures for reactive incidents: the delay each incident caused, and how long the road took to clear. This page defines both, and the timestamp-completeness badges that qualify every clearance figure. It assumes you have read the delay-attribution page.

What it answers

How much delay did incidents cause, and how quickly were they cleared? The Data & Methodology page defines roadway clearance time as "Time from incident verification to all lanes reopened — the FHWA TIM headline measure." TIM is Traffic Incident Management. Attributed delay is the event footprint defined on the delay-attribution page, summed per incident.

The scope is reactive incidents: crashes, road hazards, weather and special events. Construction, maintenance and emergency operations belong to work-zone delay.

basisEvents are TRANSCOM events filtered to New York, classified by NYSDOT sub-category. Completeness shares are of 3.7 million events, all categories, queried 2026-06-22.

How it is computed

Attributed delay

  1. Build the incident's footprint: its affected segments and 5-minute slices, with delay above the recurrent baseline, as on the delay-attribution page.
  2. Sum the footprint to vehicle-hours. Convert to dollars at the value of time the page states. See Cost of congestion.
  3. Rank, bucket by category, or sum by month and region. Events without a footprint carry a dash, not a zero.

Roadway clearance time

  1. Keep only events that carry both an incident-verified timestamp and an all-lanes-open timestamp.
  2. Subtract: all lanes open minus incident verified.
  3. Report the median, the 85th percentile and the share cleared within 90 minutes, and beside each the share of events the figure rests on.
RCT = end_all_lanes_open − incident_verified subset: both timestamps present report: median · 85th percentile · % cleared < 90 min · coverage % RCT = roadway clearance time · end_all_lanes_open = the timestamp all lanes reopened to traffic · incident_verified = the timestamp the incident was confirmed coverage % = the share of events in the filter that carry both timestamps

Duration

Incident duration, as distinct from clearance, comes from the estimated duration recorded on the event. About 99 % of events carry one. The values cluster at round estimates.

  • Clearance is computed only where the needed pair of timestamps exists. The badge on each panel restates the subset size. The Incidents dashboard's own band quotes a higher lanes-open share for its reactive subset than the all-events 42.7 % above; the two shares have different denominators (all events vs the dashboard's reactive subset), so read each badge with its own base..
  • A footprint exists on about 18.5 % of events. Delay and cost are computed only for those. The dashboard rounds this to "about one in five".
  • Category: NYSDOT sub-category, reactive set only.
  • Filters: Year is one year; Region is one NYSDOT region or statewide.

The choices made, and why

  • We headline the share cleared within 90 minutes rather than the median. Medians on a sparse subset shift as timestamp coverage improves. Threshold shares tolerate the missingness better.
  • Coverage is trended, not hidden. Each clearance figure carries a "based on N % of incidents" badge, and the completeness itself is charted, so an improving median can be read against improving coverage.
  • The lead figure is delay, not count. Most lost time comes from a small number of long, lane-blocking events. Counting events would hide that.
  • Construction is excluded so that this page describes what operations responds to, and the planned-work story lives once, on Work Zones.

How much to trust it

Attributed delay is trusted from 2019, when probe attribution coverage matured. Clearance figures are as good as the timestamps behind them: with verification present on one event in five, a region-year median can rest on a few dozen events. Read the badge first. Duration is an estimate at the time of the event, not a measurement.

Known limits

  • Most events have no measured footprint. They appear in lists and counts with a dash for delay and cost. An event list never implies every event delayed traffic.
  • Events are counted across the feed's whole history unless the page filters by year. Check the panel's own basis line.
  • Secondary crashes are not tracked. That is on the roadmap.
  • Small regions can show missing bars where a chart's axis threshold exceeds the region's delay.

Comparability across years and versions

Compare attributed delay from 2019 onward, within the current attribution series. Compare clearance figures only with their coverage badges side by side: a lower median on a year with more timestamps is not the same improvement as a lower median at equal coverage. Use Incident Search to list events by region, county, year, month and category, ranked by attributed delay, and open any event for its footprint. The event-by-segment delay table is in the Data & Methodology page's Downloads & API section.
