Truck Travel Time Reliability at the 80th percentile (TTTR₈₀).

The same truck ratio as TTTR, read at a percentile the sample can support. This is a platform measure, not a federal one. It assumes you have read the TTTR page.

measure applies to · NPMRDS Macro View (www/npmrds) methodology 2026-08 (pm3 analytical series) last reviewed · 2026-09-04 owner · —

What it answers

How unreliable truck travel is, measured at a percentile the sample can support. The same ratio as TTTR but taken at the 80th percentile instead of the 95th. Reads lower than TTTR by construction; its value is that it needs a far smaller sample for the same confidence, which matters because truck coverage is thin.

TTTR₈₀ is not a federal measure and must never be presented as one. 23 CFR 490 requires the 95th-over-50th ratio. This is its diagnostic sibling.

basisFigures on this page are CY 2025, statewide, freight trucks with an all-vehicle fallback, the full monitored network.

How it is computed

  1. Take the same truck travel-time bins, the same five periods and the same all-vehicle fallback as TTTR.
  2. Within each period, take the 80th-percentile and the 50th-percentile bin travel time.
  3. Divide. Publish per segment, per period, per year, beside TTTR rather than instead of it.
TTTR₈₀ = p80(TT) ÷ p50(TT) p80(TT) = the 80th-percentile truck travel time of the segment's 15-minute bins in the period p50(TT) = the 50th-percentile (median) truck travel time of the same bins same stream, same bins, same calculator as TTTR · read at a shallower percentile

Parameters and edge rules

  • No threshold and no target. There is no federal or state target at the 80th percentile. The Macro View deliberately shows none, because a threshold would imply a compliance reading this measure cannot carry.
  • Periods, stream and fallback are TTTR's, unchanged.
  • Minimum sample: none enforced. The ±0.05 bar is 195 bins and is published per row.

The choices made, and why

  • It exists because the federal measure is not estimable per segment on this network. TTTR's 95th percentile costs 2.4 to 4.9 times the sample of an 80th percentile for the same confidence, and truck bins reported on only 8.0 % of the network in 2020 and 22.7 % in 2025.
  • Only the percentile moves. Same truck stream, same periods, same calculator. Any difference from TTTR is purely the depth of the tail being read.
  • Published alongside TTTR rather than replacing it, so the federal figure stays available and the two can be compared directly.

How much to trust it

It reaches the ±0.05 precision bar at 195 bins, against TTTR's 57,832: 297 times cheaper. 68.8 % of CY 2025 segments clear the bar, against TTTR's 1.15 %. The measured spread of the ratio is 0.057 at 25 bins, 0.018 at 250 bins and 0.005 at 2,000 bins.

Known limits

  • Its values are lower than TTTR by construction. Never present a TTTR₈₀ figure as TTTR, and never compare it to the 2.00 target.
  • It reads a shallower tail. It is less sensitive to rare severe events. That is the point, and it means it is not a substitute when the question is worst-case freight delay.
  • It inherits TTTR's all-vehicle fallback, so it is not a pure truck measure either.

Comparability across years and versions

Use the truck era tag, as for TTTR. TTTR₈₀ was added to the analytical series in August 2026 and has no earlier published values. See Coverage eras and comparability.
