Peak periods and epochs.

The time units behind every number on the platform: the 5-minute epoch, the 15-minute reporting bin, the peak windows each measure uses, and the one label that means two different windows. Written for anyone who joins two figures by period.

concept applies to · all products last reviewed · 2026-09-05 owner · —

Epochs and reporting bins

An epoch is one 5-minute observation of travel time on one segment. It is the rawest unit in the feed. A day holds 288 epochs. A reporting bin is a 15-minute window, three epochs, averaged before any percentile is taken. Every reliability and delay measure on the Macro View is computed from bin means, not from raw epochs.

A bin counts as present when any one of its three epochs is present. That rule is why the two coverage percentages differ: epochs reporting is always lower than bins reporting. Data coverage explains the pair.

The averaging step is the largest outlier suppressor in the pipeline, and nobody chose it. A screen applied to epochs reaches 5.3 times further than the same numeric bound applied to bin means, 1.72 % of epochs against 0.33 % of bins. Binning therefore hides roughly four fifths of the extreme tail from everything downstream. It is a smoothing choice, inherited rather than decided. It is not imputation under 23 CFR 490.509(b): nothing is replaced and nothing is dropped.

noteA "no record" epoch means no probe vehicle reported on that segment in those 5 minutes. The platform does not fill it. A bin with one epoch present is a full bin; a bin with none is absent.

Resolution on the Segment page

The Segment (TMC) page design carries a Resolution control with three values: Hour, 15 min and 5 min. Resolution bins the raw epochs. At Hour, one cell is up to 12 observations averaged. The control changes what the grid and the distribution are counting, not how they are drawn. The Segment page is a design at the time of writing; its Resolution control offers Hour, 15 min and 5 min.

Corridor View replays a corridor at 5-minute resolution, from 05:00 to midnight, and drops the 00:00 to 05:00 epochs as too sparse to read. See Corridor View.

The peak windows

Each measure assigns bins to periods before it takes a percentile or a sum. The federal rule fixes the windows. The table lists every window the platform uses, with the measures that read it. Times are local, inclusive of the start and exclusive of the end.

Weekday and weekend are the first split. A Saturday afternoon hour is weekend, never midday. Overnight wins over both: a 22:00 Saturday hour is overnight. The federal reliability measures publish four periods for LOTTR and five for TTTR. PHED publishes two, the AM and the 15:00–18:59 PM peak, and the TSMO Data & Methodology page states them as "6–10 AM, 3–7 PM weekdays".

Period size sets the sample a percentile rests on. An AM peak holds 4,176 bins in CY 2025; an overnight period holds 14,600. LOTTR and TTTR publish the bar each needs.

basisWhen you quote a period figure, name the window in hours, not only its label: "PM peak, 16:00–19:59, weekdays, CY 2025".

The PM-peak label trap

"PM peak" means two different windows. LOTTR and TTTR compute their PM peak over 16:00–19:59. PHED computes its PM peak over 15:00–18:59 and publishes it under the same label. The labels match; the hours do not. Do not join a LOTTR PM-peak figure to a PHED PM-peak figure and assume the same window.

The mismatch predates the current pipeline and is not corrected, because renaming a published column would break every existing consumer. Coverage is exempt: it publishes the 16:00–19:59 and 15:00–18:59 windows as separate bins, so either denominator is unambiguous. The download package restates this in its PM3_README.txt.

Five-minute slices and fifteen-minute bins

Two families use two time units, and the difference is part of why their totals never match. The Macro View measures (LOTTR, TTTR, PHED, TED) work from 15-minute bin means over a calendar year. The excessive-delay series behind the TSMO dashboards works from 5-minute slices, summed to segment and month. A 5-minute slice keeps the extreme epochs that a bin mean smooths away, so a delay figure from the series reads differently from a TED figure for the same road, even before the threshold and the geography are considered.

The free-flow reference behind the delay family is the 15th-percentile travel time of the 15-minute bins, which is the 85th-percentile speed, taken over a fixed window rather than an off-peak set of hours. PHED documents the window. The 2024 report tool defined free flow over off-peak hours instead: "Off-peak periods Monday through Friday, 9am to 4pm and 7pm to 10pm, as well as Saturday and Sunday 6am to 10pm." That is the legacy tool's definition. The current measures do not use it.

Where this matters

Compare periods only when their hours match: a LOTTR PM peak against a TTTR PM peak is safe, against a PHED PM peak it is not. Read the bin count before you trust an overnight truck ratio. Do not expect a monthly delay total to reconcile with an annual TED figure; they count different slices. Do not apply the 2024 report tool's off-peak window to a current free-flow figure. When a Segment page grid changes with Resolution, the data did not change; the averaging did. Coverage eras and comparability covers the other axis of comparability, how much of each period actually arrived.
