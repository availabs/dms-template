Coverage and freshness.

What each data source covers, how far behind it runs, how often it refreshes and where the gaps are. Read this before you compare two years, quote a partial year, or wonder why a month shows nothing. It assumes you know which product you are looking at.

How the pages show freshness

Freshness is asymmetric on purpose. Events are live, speeds run about two weeks behind, and derived tables are batch. So every panel carries its own data-as-of badge instead of one sitewide date.

  • NPMRDS pages carry a freshness strip in the form "npmrds speeds · since Jan 2017 · complete through month · next month partial". At its page revision of 2026-08-24 the Route Comparison strip read "since jan 2017 · jun 2026 · jul 2026 partial". A month is marked partial while its data is still arriving.
  • TSMO pages carry a data-as-of badge on each panel. The TSMO home's Data Freshness band shows "Latest: date" per source. The Data & Methodology page's refresh-cadence table lists each source with its cadence and last update.
  • The Macro View's Year list is the set of published annual computations, not a free date range. Each year is complete.
  • Downloads carry a manifest that restates the coverage eras the requested years touch and flags a year range that straddles an AADT revision.

Partial years show partial totals

A year with four months of data shows four months of delay, in the same card as a full year. The TSMO Year filter offers every year with any data. At the August 2026 review it offered 2024, holding one month, and 2026, holding four months, beside 2025, the only complete year. Selecting either produced a partial-year total presented as an annual figure.

The decision taken is to expose only complete years, from the data side. Until that lands, check the months a year holds before you quote its total, and label a partial year partial: "2026, January to April". The Congestion dashboard's own caption states the rule: "Partial years show partial totals."

Why a month or a region shows $0 or nothing

  • The month has not been run. A month with no data shows nothing, not zero. In November 2025 the Congestion dashboard's month options showed nothing for October and November 2025 while every earlier month had data. Blank means not yet computed.
  • No attributed events in that region and month. Incident cost is the sum of per-event footprints, and a footprint exists on about 18.5 % of events. A region-month whose events carry no footprint shows a cost of $0. The legacy tracker recorded Region 2, August 2025, showing $0 on the Incidents dashboard, which is the shape this produces.
  • A chart threshold hides small regions. A bar chart with an axis floor of 0.1 million vehicle-hours shows no bar for a region below it. The missing bar is not a missing year.
  • Grey on the Macro View is no data, not zero. A segment with no AADT or no speed limit carries no delay and is left uncoloured. Read the "no data" count in the right panel before reading a delay map as coverage of the state.
  • 2019 and 2020 are a gap in the excessive-delay series. Trend charts show the gap rather than interpolating.

Segment counts change with the year

The Macro View's 2024 dataset holds 22,754 segment rows. Its 2025 dataset holds 52,127. Switching the Year control therefore changes the size of the network being mapped, not only its values. Whether the 2024 run is incomplete or the network changed is an open data question. Until it is answered, do not read a 2024-to-2025 change on the Macro View as a change in traffic. State both segment counts if you must compare.

Separately, the TMC network is re-cut every year. A segment may not exist in every vintage, and the 2019 expansion added about 10,000 segments. The TMC network and its vintages covers what that does to a multi-year series.

What is not computed

  • Three Macro View measures: free-flow speed, emissions and attributes. See Measures in development.
  • Excessive delay for 2019 and 2020, in the current series.
  • Roadway clearance time for any event missing either of its two timestamps (see the table below).
  • Secondary crashes, which the event feed does not identify.
  • A delay footprint for the 81.5 % of events that are not delay-relevant. Event lists never imply that every event delayed traffic.

Incident timestamp completeness

Traffic Incident Management (TIM) measures need pairs of timestamps, and most events do not carry them. Shares are of 3.7 million events, all categories, queried 2026-06-22. Each clearance panel restates the share it rests on as a badge.

TimestampShare of events carrying itWhat it gates
All lanes reopened to traffic42.7 %The end of roadway clearance time
Incident verified20.1 %The start of roadway clearance time. Clearance is computed only where this and lanes-open both exist

Response arrives on scene0.3 %The response timeline. Too sparse for a measure

Because verification exists on one event in five, a region-year clearance median can rest on a few dozen events. Incident delay and clearance explains why the dashboards headline the share cleared within 90 minutes rather than the median.
