TRANSCOM events.

The event store behind every TSMO incident, work-zone and attribution figure: incidents, construction, conditions and special events from the TRANSCOM coalition, multi-state, filtered to New York. The datasheet for operations staff and analysts, including how sparse its timestamps are.

data applies to · TSMO (www/tsmo) last reviewed · 2026-09-05 owner · —

Overview

TRANSCOM is the coalition of transportation and public-safety agencies in the New York, New Jersey and Connecticut region that shares real-time event information. The TSMO Data & Methodology page describes the store as "Incidents, construction, conditions, and special events from the TRANSCOM coalition. The feed covers the whole multi-state region — every page here filters to New York." The Data Manager catalogues it as "Transcom Events" with 60 columns and no description; the live page is the written source.

One row is one event. Events are classified by NYSDOT category at three levels: general, sub-category and detailed. The dashboards key on the sub-category. The Incidents dashboard keeps the reactive set and its Category filter offers Crash, Message, Other, Road Hazard, Special Event, Volume/Delay and Weather Hazard. The Work Zones dashboard keeps events whose sub-category is Construction, Maintenance or Emergency Operations. Landing copy that says the events are "stitched from 511NY feeds" is not the documented source; TRANSCOM is.

basisCounts on this page are the whole New York-filtered store, all categories, unless a year is named. Shares of events are of the 3.7 million events queried 2026-06-22.

When the store starts

Two live statements disagree by a few months. The TSMO Data & Methodology page states "2015 → present". The TSMO home's design header, at its 2026-06-12 data revision, counted "3.73M since Oct 2014", and the home's Incident Search card dates the store from 2014. Both are true of the same table: the earliest rows fall in October 2014, and the page that documents the source states the series as beginning in 2015. The live Data & Methodology page is the authority. Quote the span as "2015 to present"; if you need the earliest rows, say "first events October 2014, per the TSMO home". The docs pages that mention the store follow this rule.

The three NYSDOT category columns are not in the catalog's list for this source; they appear on the archived event-to-road-network table and on the production view the dashboards bind to. The production view the dashboards bind to carries all three; the Data Manager's column list for the catalogued source omits them.

Two derived tables sit beside the store. The event-by-segment delay table expands each delay-relevant event to the segments and 5-minute slices it affected, with bound start and end dates and times and the delay attributed; it is the footprint the incident page draws. The congestion attribution writes each event's vehicle-hours and cost back onto the event row. See Delay attribution.

How it is produced

  1. The coalition feed is ingested continuously and filtered to New York. June 2026 held 36,574 New York events at the 2026-07-15 check.
  2. Events are classified into NYSDOT's general, sub- and detailed categories.
  3. Each month, delay-relevant events are matched to segments through the network conflation and attributed a footprint by replaying speeds around the event. A footprint exists on 18.5 % of events, by design; event lists never imply that every event delayed traffic.
  4. The attribution runs in batch behind the live feed, so a recent event can show no footprint yet.

Uses and limits

The store drives the Incidents and Work Zones dashboards, Incident Search, the incident page, and the construction, accident and other buckets of the excessive-delay series. Its limits are the feed's. Timestamps are sparse, so clearance time exists for one event in five and a region-year median can rest on a few dozen events; the dashboards headline the share cleared within 90 minutes instead. A region-month whose events carry no footprint shows a cost of $0. "3.7M events since 2015" on a dashboard counts the whole feed, all years; every panel below it follows the selected year. Multi-state rows are present in the store and filtered out on every page.
