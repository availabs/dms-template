Macro View: how to use.

One map, every segment in the state, one measure at a time. The Macro View is where an analysis starts: rank a region, see where a measure is worst, and export exactly what the map is drawing. This page walks the whole tool in the order you would use it. Reading is public.

how-to applies to · NPMRDS last reviewed · 2026-09-05 owner · —

Before you start

Open Macro View from the NPMRDS side navigation. No sign-in is needed. The page is one map with a controls panel, a measure panel, and a bottom bar that names the data behind the map and opens the download.

The Macro View answers "where is this measure worst, across a whole region?", and it is the fastest way to export a measure for every segment in a geography. It is a starting point, not a finishing one: once you know which segments matter, the other tools go deeper.

When another tool is the right one:

Steps

  1. Open Measure and pick one. The menu is grouped: reliability, congestion, speed, data quality.The controls below the menu change to the ones that measure uses, and a count such as 1 control or 4 controls says how many.
  2. Set the measure's own controls: Peak period for the reliability measures, Threshold · Unit · Traffic type for the delay measures, Percentile for speed, Basis for coverage.
  3. Under Geography, type into add a region, county or MPO… and pick from Regions, MPOs, Urban areas or Counties. Leave it empty for the whole state.A chip appears. The map, the distribution, the worst-segments list and the download all narrow to it.
  4. Pick a Year. The list is the set of published PM3 years, newest first.
  5. Read the measure panel: the definition, the legend, Value distribution, Median, 80th pctl, the share beyond the threshold, and the no-data count.
  6. Switch the measure to Coverage · data completeness once before you trust a pattern, then switch back.
  7. Open Worst 25 segments to rank the current measure within the current geography.The list fills and the same 25 segments are drawn as points on the map, coloured by their legend bin.
  8. Select Download N rows in the bottom bar to export what the map is drawing. Download data from the Macro View has the builder in full.
  9. Copy the address from the browser to share the view.

The control model

Pick the measure first. The controls below it then change. Every measure declares which reference controls apply to it, so the panel never shows a control that does nothing. The panel tells you how many are in play: note the count under the measure select.

why so many segments go grey on PHEDDelay needs traffic volume and a speed reference, and a large share of the network has neither. Those segments are not coloured rather than coloured zero. An absent measure and a measure of zero are different claims, and the map keeps them different. Check the no-data count in the measure panel before reading a delay map as coverage of the whole state.

Geography and year

Geography. Type into add a region, county or MPO… to filter. The picker groups its options as Regions, MPOs, Urban areas and Counties, and several can be added. Leave it empty and you get the whole state, which is the right choice when you want the statewide distribution behind the legend. Adding a geography narrows the map, the distribution summary, the worst-segments ranking and the download all at once.

Year. The year list is the set of published PM3 years, not a free date range. Each is a complete annual computation. In the View control, Single year is the only mode: Compare years is drawn but disabled, and its tooltip says it is planned. Before comparing two years by hand, read Coverage eras and comparability and AADT and its vintage: both move across years for reasons that have nothing to do with traffic, and the 2024 dataset holds fewer than half the segments of 2025.

Reading the map and the measure panel

The measure panel is the measure's own explainer, the same definition text the measure pages use, plus the legend and the distribution of the values on screen.

Legend
Seven fixed bins whose edges do not move with the year or the geography. For LOTTR and TTTR the edges are anchored to the federal tests, 1.50 and 2.00, so the colours mean the same thing everywhere. For delay the edges are round decades, because delay spans orders of magnitude; for speed they are 10 mph steps. The top bin is open-ended.
Value distribution
A histogram of what is currently on the map, sharing the legend's scale, with the threshold marked. It is the fastest check that a filter did what you meant.
Summary figures
Median, 80th pctl, and the share beyond the threshold, labelled Unreliable for LOTTR and Above target for TTTR. They describe the current filter, not the state, unless the filter is empty.
No data
Counted, stated as no data · not coloured, and drawn uncoloured. Grey is not zero.
Get to a segment
Type at least three characters of a TMC code or a county name into TMC or county… and pick a result. The map zooms to that segment. The search does not know road names.
Hover
Hovering a segment shows its TMC code, the current measure's value, the county and the region. The road name is not on the hover.

Check coverage before you trust a pattern

Coverage is a measure in the menu like any other, and it is the one worth looking at first when something surprising shows up. A region that looks unusually unreliable may simply have less data behind it. Its Basis control offers Bins reporting, the share of a measure's own 15-minute input that arrived, and Epochs reporting, the share of the raw 5-minute feed, which is lower by construction.

Worst segments

Worst 25 segments in the measure panel ranks the current measure over the current filter. The row says in current filter for exactly that reason. Set your geography first, or you will rank the whole state. Opening the list also draws the same 25 segments as points on the map, so a ranking is visible on the canvas, not only in the list.

two cautions before you act on a rankingShort segments dominate reliability rankings. 24.3 % of segments are under 0.05 mi and are flagged unreliable 7.7 times more often than long ones, so a top 25 by LOTTR can be mostly very short segments. Delay rankings favour high-volume roads by construction, because delay is multiplied by traffic. That is usually what you want, but it is not the same question as "where is traffic slowest".

Downloading the data

The Download N rows button carries a row count so you know the size before you ask for it. What you get is exactly what the map is drawing: same filters, same year, same bins. Choose a scope, Current filters or Statewide, a format, csv or gpkg, and whether to include geometry; Columns you'll get lists the columns before you commit. Every package ships a README naming the year's coverage era, the free-flow reference window, the delay floor and the AADT caveat. Download data from the Macro View covers the builder, the package and how to cite it.

Sharing a view

The address is the state. Copy it from the browser and whoever opens it sees what you saw. Two rules govern it, and both are deliberate.

Only what you changed is written. A fresh Macro View leaves the address clean. Parameters appear only as you move away from the defaults, so a shared link is a readable description of your choices rather than a wall of state.

Unknown values degrade, they do not break. Every value in a link is checked against the live vocabulary and quietly dropped if it no longer resolves. An old link whose measure or year has since retired still opens; it falls back to the default rather than erroring.

Because of the second rule, a shared link is not a permanent citation. If a figure matters, download it: the export is fixed, the link is live.

Limits and gotchas

  • Three measures the menu once promised are not computed for the current dataset and are kept out of the menu: free-flow speed, emissions and attributes. Measures in development explains.
  • The hover and the search know the TMC code, not the road name; the dataset behind the map carries no road name, length or AADT. Asked in September 2026; see References.
  • There is no per-mile control and no filter by NHS status or functional class. Both were requested in August 2026 and are open.
  • The 2024 dataset holds 22,754 segments against 52,127 for 2025, so changing Year changes the network, not only the values. The 2024 segment count records the open question.
  • The Macro View shows the analytical series. For a federal figure use the MAP-21 PM3 pages; Federal PM3 and what this site publishes says why the two differ.

Troubleshooting

The page showed an error on first load

"Unable to complete your request at the moment. Please try again later." was reported on 2026-09-03; the second attempt succeeded. Reload. If it repeats, report it with the time.

A shared link opened on the default view

A value in the link no longer resolves, most often a year or a measure, and was dropped. Re-select and share again. To keep a figure, download it.

The map is mostly grey

You are on a delay measure or on trucks, where most segments have no value. Read the no data · not coloured count, then check Coverage · data completeness for the same geography.
