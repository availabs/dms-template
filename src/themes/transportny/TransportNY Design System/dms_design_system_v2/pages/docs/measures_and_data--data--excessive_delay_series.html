Excessive-delay series.

The segment-by-month delay table behind the TSMO Congestion dashboard and the TSMO home: total excessive delay per segment and month, split into recurrent and non-recurrent, and the non-recurrent part into construction, accident and other. For analysts who download it or need to know which months it holds.

data applies to · TSMO (www/tsmo) methodology · excessive delay, June 2026 revision last reviewed · 2026-09-05 owner · —

Overview

The series is one row per TMC segment per calendar month, in vehicle-hours. The TSMO Data & Methodology page describes it as "Our TMC × month delay table — replays speeds around events to split delay into cause buckets. The engine behind the Congestion and Work Zones dashboards. 3.4M TMC-month rows · 2018 + 2021–2025 · buckets: recurrent / non-recurrent / construction / accident / other." It is derived in-house from the travel-time table, the network and the TRANSCOM events.

The measure is documented on Excessive delay and the split on Delay attribution. The current series is the June 2026 revision: threshold at the greater of 20 mph and 60 % of the posted limit, median weekday baseline, capped attribution. It is not comparable with figures published before June 2026, which used a flat 20 mph threshold and a mean baseline.

basisStatewide, all vehicles, the monitored network with a posted speed limit and an AADT, vehicle-hours, per segment and month. Dollar figures are a conversion made by the page, not a stored value.

Schema

The columns the live Congestion dashboard and TSMO home bind to. The series is not publicly readable in the Data Manager, so the list is the pages' bound columns; segment attributes are joined from the network of the row's year.

FieldTypeSample valueDescription
tmcstring—The TMC segment id.
year · monthinteger2025 · 10Calendar year and month of the row.
totalnumber—Total excessive delay in the month, vehicle-hours: time below the threshold times vehicles present, summed over 5-minute slices.
non_recurrentnumber—Delay above the segment's median weekday baseline, vehicle-hours. Recurrent delay is total minus non_recurrent; it is not stored.
construction · accident · othernumber—Non-recurrent delay attributed to overlapping TRANSCOM events by category, vehicle-hours. The three sum to at most non_recurrent.
region_code · region_name · county_codestring—NYSDOT region and county, from the network. The Region filter keys on region_code.
roadname · direction · road_informationstring—Road name, direction and descriptive text; the corridor table groups on roadname.
lengthnumber—Segment length in miles.
f_systeminteger—Functional system, 1 to 7, 0 for the extended set.
aadt · aadt_singl · aadt_combinumber—AADT and the two truck classes, for the class-weighted value of time. aadt here is the directional, facility-normalised figure the delay used.
tmclinear · road_orderinteger · number—Corridor chain keys, from the network.
wkb_geometry · ogc_fidgeometry · integer—Segment line and row id.

Two columns designed in June 2026, an effective value of time per row (vot_eff) and a cost, are not on the live table at the 2026-07-15 check. Pages compute dollars themselves: the Congestion dashboard at an interim flat $20 per vehicle-hour, the Data & Methodology page at the class-weighted rates. See Cost of congestion.

How it is produced

  1. Each month's TRANSCOM events are attributed a congestion footprint first, by replaying speeds around each event on the conflated network.
  2. The month's 5-minute travel times are compared with the segment's threshold and its median weekday baseline; delay is weighted by directional AADT spread across the day by a fixed profile.
  3. Non-recurrent delay is split across the events that overlap the segment and month; bucket sums are capped at the segment-month's non-recurrent delay.
  4. Because the fill ran backwards, each year's attribution is re-run once its neighbouring months exist, so events crossing a month boundary are not missed.
  5. A month takes about 1.3 to 2 hours of attribution and about a minute of delay computation; dashboards are re-seeded when a period lands.

Uses and limits

Use it for where, when and why delay occurs: region-month totals, the recurrent share, construction against crash delay, corridor rankings. Do not compare it with the Macro View's TED or PHED, which use 15-minute bins, an annual scope and a free-flow reference; do not compare it with figures published before June 2026; do not add 2019 or 2020 from the older series, which is not comparable. A partial year shows a partial total in the same card as a full year. A month with no rows shows nothing. Delay is linear in AADT and inherits its revision cycle; see AADT and its vintage.
