Start here

One guided task of about 10 minutes: a year, three numbers, a dashboard. You open the TSMO home, pick a year and read the headline cards with what each one counts. Then you open the Congestion dashboard, set a region, find the worst corridors and open one in Corridor View. No account is needed.

If you are about to quote a number, read Federal PM3 and what this site publishes first. The reliability figures are federal. The delay figures are the platform's own series, revised in June 2026.

Common tasks

  • Read a statewide figure, then the same figure for one region, and get back to statewide.
  • Find out what "statewide" means when Region is blank, and why the regions do not add up to it.
  • Find the worst corridors for a year and region.
  • Quote a CY 2025 reliability figure with its year, system and target.
  • Find an incident by region, county, date, category or keyword, and read its page.
  • Read a map that shows a pin but no segment.
  • Replay a corridor on one day, and find out why your region did not follow you there.
  • Find out why a month or a region shows $0 or nothing.
  • Check whether a year is partial before quoting its total.
  • Get the delay data out through the Data & Methodology page.

What each page answers

One line per live page. The six dashboard and explorer lines are the About TSMO page's own dashboard guide, verbatim.

  • TSMO home · the headline numbers for a selected year, the door to every dashboard and explorer, and how fresh each source is.
  • Congestion · "how much delay, where, and why".
  • Reliability · "how predictable is travel".
  • Incidents · "what happens on the network & how well we clear it".
  • Work Zones · "what construction costs drivers".
  • Incident Search · "find any of 3.7M events since 2015".
  • Incident View · one event's identity, its response timeline, the delay it added above the recurrent baseline, and where.
  • Corridor View · "replay any corridor, any day".
  • About TSMO · what TSMO is, how NYSDOT's program is framed, what each page answers, and which measures are not built yet and why.
  • Data & Methodology · where every number comes from, how each measure is computed, where the data is thin, how often each feed refreshes.
