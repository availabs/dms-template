Cost of congestion.

Delay in vehicle-hours converted to dollars at a value of time. Two bases exist on the site today, a flat $20 per vehicle-hour and a vehicle-class-weighted rate. This page says which is which and which pages show each. It assumes you have read the excessive-delay page.

measure applies to · TSMO dashboards (www/tsmo) methodology · excessive delay, June 2026 revision last reviewed · 2026-09-04 owner · —

What it answers

What the delay on the network is worth in dollars. The Data & Methodology page defines the current basis as "Delay monetized at a vehicle-class-weighted value of time. Each segment's vehicle mix comes from its AADT split — passenger cars, single-unit trucks, and combination trucks — and each class carries its own per-vehicle rate."

Cost is a conversion of excessive delay or of an event's attributed delay. It adds no new measurement. Every caveat on those pages carries over.

basisRates are in 2024–25 dollars per vehicle-hour, with vehicle occupancy already included. Delay figures on this page are CY 2025, statewide, all vehicles, the monitored network.

How it is computed

The class-weighted basis (current method)

  1. Take the segment's AADT and its single-unit and combination truck counts. Passenger AADT is the remainder.
  2. Weight the three class rates by their shares to get the segment's effective value of time.
  3. Multiply the segment's delay in vehicle-hours by that rate. For an event, sum the product over the segments in its footprint.
  4. Where a segment's AADT split is missing or zero, use the network-blend rate rather than dropping the segment.
VOT_eff = pass% × $52 + singl% × $42 + combi% × $77 cost = delay_vh × VOT_eff VOT_eff = the segment's effective value of time, in dollars per vehicle-hour pass% · singl% · combi% = the segment's share of passenger cars, single-unit trucks and combination trucks, from its AADT split delay_vh = delay in vehicle-hours · fallback when the split is missing: the network blend, about $52 per vehicle-hour

The flat basis (earlier method, still shown on some pages)

cost = delay_vh × $20 $20 = a flat value of time per vehicle-hour, described on the Congestion dashboard as FHWA's value of time

Parameters and edge rules

Vehicle classRate per vehicle-hourBasis
Passenger vehicles$52Maryland CHART 2022 car rate of $46.50, occupants included, escalated by the consumer price index
Single-unit trucks (FHWA classes 4–7)$42Bureau of Labor Statistics driver wage plus lighter cargo. New York's single-unit class is bus-heavy, so this is the least clean rate
Combination trucks (FHWA classes 8–13)$77CHART driver $23.41 plus cargo $45.40, escalated by the consumer price index; one occupant
Network blend (fallback)about $52New York's AADT-weighted mix, roughly $50–55; $58–62 on freight corridors with 15–20 % combination trucks
  • Do not multiply by occupancy again. The rates are per vehicle-hour with occupants bundled. The earlier method double-counted occupancy on some pages.
  • Rates are versioned. A future escalation creates a new rate version rather than changing this one, so an old dashboard stays reproducible.

The choices made, and why

  • We replaced the flat $20 with a class-weighted rate, adopted 2026-06-19 and confirmed 2026-06-22, because a truck-heavy freight corridor and a commuter parkway are not worth the same per vehicle-hour. The blend is about 2.5 times the old rate, so dollar figures roughly 2.5 times higher are a rate change, not a traffic change.
  • Weighting is per segment from its own AADT split, which the network table already carries, so no new data was needed.
  • Missing splits fall back to the blend rather than to zero. A segment is never dropped for lacking a truck count.

How much to trust it

A dollar figure inherits the uncertainty of the delay behind it and adds the rate assumption. The rates are point estimates from two published sources, escalated once. The single-unit rate is the weakest: New York's single-unit class carries buses with 10.7 to 16.8 occupants, so a transit value of time may suit bus-dominated urban segments better. That question is open.

Which pages show which basis

The rate change is applied at the data layer when the delay series and the event footprints are recomputed. Until that recomputation is published, pages show the figure their data carries.

Worked example

CY 2025, statewide, all vehicles, flat basis: 310.86 million vehicle-hours × $20 = $6.2 billion, the figure the Congestion dashboard and the TSMO home carried at the June 2026 data revision. At the class-weighted blend the same delay is worth roughly 2.5 times as much; the exact statewide figure is not yet published; it appears here when the recomputed series is.

Known limits

  • Two bases coexist. A dollar figure from one page is not comparable to a dollar figure from another unless both name the same rate.
  • A month or region can show $0. Cost is delay times a rate, so a month with no delay data shows $0 or nothing. Region 2, August 2025 showed $0 on Incidents for this reason.
  • The rates are national or Maryland sources escalated to New York, not New York-specific surveys.
  • Cost is not a benefit estimate. It values time lost; it does not net out anything.

Comparability across years and versions

Compare dollars only within one rate version and one delay series. A series recomputed at the class-weighted rate is not comparable to figures published at $20, and neither is comparable to the delay series in use before June 2026. Read Methodology changes for the dated switch.

Reproducibility

Download the delay table for the year from the Data & Methodology page's Downloads & API section. Recompute cost as delay times the rate you intend to cite, and say which rate. Once published, the recomputed series carries an effective value-of-time column per segment-month.
