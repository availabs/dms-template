Total Excessive Delay (TED).

Vehicle-hours lost below a threshold speed over all hours of the year. TED is the all-hours sibling of PHED and shares its threshold, reference and floor. Read the PHED page first; this page covers what differs.

What it answers

How many vehicle-hours are lost below the delay threshold speed. Excessive delay is the extra time spent under a speed threshold — 20 mph or 60% of the reference speed, whichever is greater.

TED is not a federal measure. It is the platform's all-hours, vehicle-hour form of the federal excessive-delay calculation. PHED is this measure restricted to peak hours and weighted by occupancy.

basisFigures on this page are CY 2025, statewide, all vehicles, the segments that qualify for the delay family, unless stated.

How it is computed

  1. Set the threshold speed and threshold travel time exactly as for PHED: the greater of 20 mph and 60 % of the reference speed.
  2. For every 15-minute bin of the year, all hours and all days, take the excess travel time over the threshold, floored at zero.
  3. Multiply by the vehicles in the bin: directional AADT times the bin's share of daily traffic.
  4. Sum over the year. Report in vehicle-hours, optionally per mile of segment.
threshold = max(0.6 × reference, 20 mph) TED = Σ (TT − TT_threshold) × volume reference = the posted speed limit or a free-flow speed, by variant (see the PHED page) TT = the observed travel time of the bin · TT_threshold = segment length ÷ threshold speed · negative excess counts as zero volume = directional AADT × the bin's share of daily traffic · Σ = the sum over every bin of the year

The same four variants exist as for PHED: posted speed limit, anchored free-flow (default), relative (unfloored) and own-year free-flow (deprecated). The Macro View's threshold, per mile and AADT source controls select them.

Parameters and edge rules

  • Period: all hours, all days of the calendar year.
  • Unit: vehicle-hours. No occupancy factor is applied.
  • Floor, reference, AADT gate and anchor fallback: identical to PHED.
  • AADT source: a Macro View control. Within one year there is one vintage, so single-year comparisons are unaffected by the choice.

The choices made, and why

  • TED shares PHED's threshold, reference choice and floor. Everything on the PHED page applies here.
  • Reported in vehicle-hours by default, because an all-hours figure has no single defensible occupancy assumption.
  • Extreme travel times are not screened away. A heavy tail is what congestion looks like. Absolute speed screens were rejected because a screen below 5 mph removes 23.16 % of the state's real congestion: 3 mph in a jam is the measurement.

How much to trust it

No precision band, for the same reason as PHED. TED is, however, the measure most exposed to outliers of anything published here. Grouping segments by their own tail weight moves median TED across a 37-fold range, against LOTTR's near-immunity. The floor figures on the PHED page apply: 62.7 % of measured delay is computed against 20 mph.

Known limits

  • A delay trend is partly an AADT revision history. Delay scales linearly in volume, and volume comes from an estimated AADT dataset with its own revision cycle. On a fixed panel of identical segments, total directional AADT fell 14.9 % between 2021 and 2022 and rose 2.3 % in 2020. Traffic did not do that in 2020; a revision cycle did. The threshold was stable at 27.759 mph in every one of the nine years.
  • Same 61 % absence as PHED. A statewide total covers the 39 % of mileage that qualifies.
  • Never compare across functional classes in a floored variant.

Comparability across years and versions

Use the anchored variant, exclude anchor-fallback rows, normalise by AADT, and check the all-vehicle era tag on both years. Coverage biases delay and reliability in opposite directions, so agreement between a TED trend and a LOTTR trend does not corroborate either across years. See Coverage eras and comparability and AADT and its vintage.
