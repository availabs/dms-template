What TransportNY is.

NYSDOT's open platform for travel-time, operations and freight data: its three products, who uses each, what is public, and where the numbers come from.

One platform, three products

TransportNY is a program of the New York State Department of Transportation (NYSDOT), in partnership with AVAIL. It started as an NYSDOT internal reporting tool and grew into a shared platform. NYSDOT uses it to file its MAP-21 PM3 federal reports. The state's 14 metropolitan planning organizations (MPOs) use it to draft their transportation improvement programs (TIPs).

Three products share one landing page, one sign-in and one docs site. The product switcher moves you between them.

The three products

NPMRDS: speed, travel time and reliability

The National Performance Management Research Data Set (NPMRDS) is the probe travel-time data set the Federal Highway Administration (FHWA) licenses to state DOTs. The NPMRDS product turns it into measures for every road segment. It answers: how fast, how reliable and how congested is this road, and how has that changed? It has four tools. Reports charts one route over time. The Macro View shows one measure, one year, every segment. Route Comparison sets routes against periods. The MAP-21 PM3 pages carry the federal figures. NYSDOT planners and MPO analysts use it most. Read the NPMRDS hub.

TSMO: operations dashboards

Transportation Systems Management and Operations (TSMO) is the practice of managing the road network day to day. The TSMO product answers: how did congestion, reliability, incidents and work zones look in a year, statewide or in one NYSDOT region? It has four dashboards: Congestion, Reliability, Incidents and Work Zones. Two explorers, Incident Search and Corridor View, sit beside About TSMO and Data & Methodology. Operations engineers, regional staff and MPO planners use it. Read the TSMO hub.

Freight Atlas: the State Freight Plan as a map

The Freight Atlas answers: where are New York's freight networks, facilities and flows, and what does the 2024 State Freight Plan say about them? It is one map with switchable layers, a Maps Gallery of the plan's figures, a Data & Downloads catalog, and About & The Plan. It serves the public as well as planners. Read the Freight Atlas hub.

What is public and what needs an account

Everything that reads is public. Sign in to save routes and reports, and for administration: uploading data and building maps. One sign-in covers the landing page, TSMO and the Freight Atlas. NPMRDS runs on its own subdomain and may ask you to sign in again.

Where the numbers come from

Three sources feed the platform. Measures & Data documents each.

NPMRDS travel times
Probe travel times averaged to 5-minute intervals on each segment of the National Highway System (NHS) and the expanded network NYSDOT licenses beyond it. Speed, reliability and delay measures come from here. See NPMRDS travel times.
TRANSCOM events
Incidents, work zones and other road events reported through TRANSCOM, the multi-state coalition of transportation agencies whose live feed carries incidents, construction, road conditions and special events. The platform keeps the New York events. TSMO's incident and work-zone pages use it. See TRANSCOM events.
The 2024 State Freight Plan
The plan's maps and the datasets behind them: freight networks, facilities and commodity flows. The Freight Atlas layers use it. See Freight Atlas sources.

Reliability appears in two forms: the MAP-21 / PM3 (federal) figures NYSDOT submits to FHWA, and the analytical series the platform computes itself. Federal PM3 and what this site publishes explains the difference.

Where this matters

When you quote a number, say which product it came from and on what basis: geography, period, vehicle class and road system. For a federal document, take the figure from the MAP-21 PM3 pages, not from a dashboard. Which tool answers my question? routes each common question to its tool.
