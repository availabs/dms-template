The 2024 State Freight Plan and the Freight Atlas.

What the plan says, in the frame the Freight Atlas gives it: six goals, 20 strategies, the report library, what changed since 2019, and how a map layer or a home-page figure relates to the printed plan. For anyone who needs to cite the plan and the site together.

Six goals

The 2024 New York State Freight Plan is NYSDOT's statewide freight plan. The Freight Atlas is its interactive companion, and the About & The Plan page carries the plan's frame in the plan's own words. The plan sets six goals, each with a one-line aim:

  • Safety · "Reduce freight-related fatalities & serious injuries".
  • Mobility · "Reliable, efficient movement of goods".
  • Condition · "State of good repair across modes".
  • Environment · "Cut emissions; build resilience (CLCPA)", the Climate Leadership and Community Protection Act.
  • Economy · "Competitiveness & freight-reliant jobs".
  • Stewardship · "Sound investment, partnership & data".

The plan's headline numbers frame the goals. In the 2021 base year New York moved 936.5 million tons of freight across all five modes, forecast to grow 34 % by 2050. The goods were worth $1.29 trillion, $1,293.7 billion, forecast to grow 57 to 67 % by 2050. Freight-reliant industries account for more than 18 % of state gross domestic product and 25 % of New York jobs, in the third-largest state economy at $2.1 trillion. By tons the modes split truck 68.6 %, water 17.0 %, rail 10.7 %, pipeline 3.2 %, air 0.3 %; by value truck 75.9 %, water 5.6 %, rail 14.7 %, pipeline 0.5 %, air 3.0 %.

Twenty strategies in five focus areas

The plan implements the goals through 20 strategies in five focus areas: Planning & Information Sharing, Operations & Maintenance, Technology, System Investment, and Engagement & Collaboration. Each strategy is mapped to the six goals in a matrix. Two strategies are the Freight Atlas itself: Strategy 3, a centralized data platform, and Strategy 18, public education. That is why the site exists, and why its pages are written to be cited.

The report library

The plan's documents are published by NYSDOT at its freight plan reports page, dot.ny.gov/freight-plan/reports, and the About & The Plan page links there rather than hosting copies. The page's own words: "The 2024 Freight Plan, executive summary, technical appendices, working-group materials, and prior plans are published by NYSDOT — every download is the current version, straight from the source." Until 2026-08-05 the page listed five hosted copies; a reader asked whether they were fresh, and NYSDOT chose the single link so that they always are.

The document set the plan comprises, as held in the platform's reference library: the 2024 main report, the executive summary, appendices A to F, four Freight Working Group meeting decks from October 2023 to February 2024, and the 2019 plan with its technical white paper. The Maps Gallery's tiles cite the main report by figure number and page.

Two entries need a gloss. FCHN and FCRN are the Freight Core Highway Network and the Freight Core Rail Network, both created in 2019 and both on the map. The "216+47" of the truck parking study is quoted as the page prints it; the home's "263 parking sites" is their sum, and the appendix names what each of the two counts is; the site prints only the sum. The "ETC index" is quoted as the plan prints it; the plan's equity chapter defines it.

How the Freight Atlas relates to the plan

The home calls the site "the living, interactive companion to the 2024 New York State Freight Plan". The plan is a fixed document with a date. The Freight Atlas is the same analysis kept live: every road, rail, port, airport, pipeline and truck-parking layer the plan analyses, the data behind them to download, and the plan's own maps as presets. Three relationships matter when you cite either.

Figures are indexed to sources. Each of the plan's 33 maps is indexed to the datasets it used and to the map layers that draw them. The Maps Gallery shows the 22 that the map can draw today; the other 11 wait for processing or data, as Maps Gallery sets out. A tile draws the live layer, which can carry a later vintage than the printed figure.

The commodity data is TRANSEARCH. S&P Global's TRANSEARCH is the commodity-flow database behind the plan's tonnage and value figures and behind the map's Transearch layers. The plan uses the 2023 release with a 2021 base year and a 2050 forecast. Write it as "TRANSEARCH 2021 base year (2023 release)", never "TRANSEARCH 2023" alone: the home once did, readers took 2023 for the data year, and the line was reworded on 2026-07-31. The 2019 plan used the 2012 base year with a 2040 forecast, so its figures are not comparable with the 2024 plan's.

Where the plan publishes a figure, the site shows the plan's figure. The rule dates from 2026-08-25, when the home's mode-share chart computed shares live from the TRANSEARCH table and disagreed with the plan. It now reads the plan's five mode shares, quoted above, with the caption "2024 NYS Freight Plan · 2021 base year · figures as published". The map layers still draw the underlying data, and one home chart, Growth to 2050, still computes its totals from TRANSEARCH, so its deltas can differ a little from the cards' "+34 %" and "+57–67 %". A headline is the plan's; a layer's value is the dataset's.

The About & The Plan page also names who makes the site: NYSDOT, with the University at Albany, the team the 2024 plan names as modernizing the "Freight Web Atlas", the plan's name for the earlier site. Corrections, dataset requests and public comments go to NYSDOT Freight Planning at the address the page gives.

Where this matters

  • Quoting a number. Take a headline from the home or the plan and say "2021 base year"; take a layer's value from its dataset and say the dataset and version. The figures agree with the plan.
  • Reading a gallery tile. Its figure number and page point into the main report; compare legends and vintages before comparing values. Maps Gallery.
  • Reading the equity and climate layers. Disadvantaged Populations (DAC Rank) is the plan's equity overlay; the plan's Environment goal is framed by the CLCPA. Layer reference.
  • Finding the documents. The NYSDOT reports page is the only source the site points to. The report library.
  • Comparing with 2019. Different base year, different forecast year, a network 4,497 miles longer. What changed.
