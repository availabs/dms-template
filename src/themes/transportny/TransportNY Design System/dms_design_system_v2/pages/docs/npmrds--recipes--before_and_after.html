Recipe: a before/after study.

Did a project change how a corridor performs? One saved route, two date windows, a Difference graph of hours of delay, and a cost at a stated value of time. The worked case is the 2024 study of the Route 787 raised intersections in Cohoes, made reproducible with today's tools. Assumes you can create a route and build a report.

recipe applies to · NPMRDS last reviewed · 2026-09-07 owner · — requires sign-in

The question

"We rebuilt this road. Did drivers save time, and how much is that worth?" In 2019 NYSDOT installed raised pavement at the intersection of Route 787 and Dyke Road, part of a $15 million project to convert Route 787, Cohoes Boulevard, into a pedestrian-friendly gateway. The 2024 write-up compared an average day before the project, labelled 2016 to 2018, with an average day after, labelled 2021 to 2023, on the northbound direction. The method is the same one the platform runs today: hours of delay below a threshold speed, multiplied by traffic, summed.

basisThe 2024 figures: Route 787 (Cohoes Boulevard) northbound, Cohoes, Albany County · before 2016–2018, after 2021–2023 as the write-up labels them · all vehicles · hours of delay against the greater of 20 mph and 60 % of the posted limit, weighted by AADT · cost at $15 per vehicle-hour. Today's tools: the same route drawn on the 2026 map, dates you set, the report Hours of Delay measure.

What you need

  • Sign-in. Saving the route and the report both write to your account.
  • Route Creation, to draw the corridor once.
  • A report, with the same route added twice.
  • Two date windows you can defend: a "before" that ends before construction began and an "after" that starts once traffic settled. The platform's travel times begin in January 2017, so a before window today starts no earlier than that. The 2024 study's 2016 data is not on the platform.
  • A value of time, stated. Cost of congestion has the platform's current class-weighted rates and the flat rate some pages still show.

Steps

  1. Open Reports and select New route. In TMC Click mode, click the northbound segments of Route 787 through Cohoes in order, from the southern end of the project limits to the northern end. Save the route with a name that carries the direction, for example "NY 787 NB · Cohoes Blvd", and your agency tag. The 2024 write-up names Route 787, Cohoes Boulevard, at Dyke Road but records no segment list; build the route along Cohoes Boulevard through Cohoes and record its extent in the description.
  2. Select Create Report.A new report opens in edit mode with the Routes rail.
  3. Select Add Route, find the route you saved, and add it. Rename the row with the pencil to "Before · 2017–2018" and set From and To to the before window.
  4. Select Add Route again, add the same route, rename it "After · 2021–2023" and set its window.Two rows of one route, each with its own dates and colour. Names must be unique within the report.
  5. Select Add Graph. Tick both rows under Routes for this graph. Choose Line Graph, Measure Avg. Hours of Delay, Resolution Hour, Comparison Mode Plain, and under When All Day and Weekdays. Select Add Graph.Two lines, one per window: average vehicle-hours of delay per weekday by hour. This is the write-up's "average day" chart.
  6. Add a second graph: the same two rows, Bar Graph, Measure Avg. Hours of Delay, Resolution Hour, Comparison Mode Difference, Anchor Route the before row.One series: before minus after, by hour. A positive bar is delay removed; a negative bar is delay added.
  7. Add a third graph: both rows, Bar Graph, Measure Hours of Delay, Resolution Summary (one bar per route), Plain.Two bars: total vehicle-hours of delay in each window. These are the totals the cost is computed from.
  8. Add a fourth graph: both rows, Bar Graph, Measure Travel Time (min), Summary.Average traversal time per window, the write-up's 2 min 47 s against 2 min 38 s.
  9. Fill the header: a title, a one-line purpose that names the project and the two windows, and the value of time you will apply. Select Done.The report publishes at its address; readers see the cards without the rail.
  10. Compute the cost in the report's purpose line or your document: divide each window's total hours of delay by its length in years, multiply by the rate, and subtract. State the rate.

Reading the result

The Difference card is the finding. With the before row as anchor, positive bars are hours of delay the corridor no longer loses. The 2024 study found the reduction concentrated in the evening rush northbound, where most of the delay had been. A change spread evenly across the day is more likely traffic growth or a data-coverage shift than a project effect; a change in one period on one direction is a project shape.

Totals become dollars at a rate you name. The write-up used $15 per vehicle-hour: 60,000 hours before and 36,250 after over its two windows became $451,000 and $271,875 per year, a saving of $179,250 per year, about 40 % less delay. The platform's current basis is a class-weighted value of time, $52 for passenger vehicles, $42 for single-unit trucks and $77 for combination trucks, blending to about $53 on the network; some TSMO pages still show a flat $20. Any of these gives a different dollar figure from the same hours. Report hours first, then dollars with the rate.

Three things move between 2017 and 2023 besides the project. The TMC map is re-cut every year, so a route drawn on the 2026 map can lose or split a segment in early years; check the segment count on both rows. Coverage moved through several eras; a thinner feed deflates delay. And AADT, the volume term in every delay figure, has its own revision cycle. Coverage eras and comparability and AADT and its vintage say how much each can move a figure. A signalised boulevard such as Route 787 also rises and falls with its signals, so windows of a year or more are the right length.

Say what else changed. The 2024 write-up asked whether signal retiming or a changed speed limit explained part of the result, and could not separate them. Neither can the data alone. Name the confounders you know of.

Variations

  • The Single Route template. If your "after" is the current year and your "before" is three years earlier, open the Single Route template with the route instead of building a report: it draws 3 Years Ago, Current Year and Trailing 3 Years, including a difference grid by segment and hour.
  • Where on the corridor. Add a Grid Graph in Difference mode: segments down, hours across, colour is before minus after. A band along a stretch is a real change; a speckle is noise.
  • Both directions. Save the southbound route too and repeat. A project that helps one direction and hurts the other is a common finding.
  • Speed instead of delay. Use Speed (mph) at Hour for a corridor where volume is thin and delay is deflated by coverage.
  • An incident or a closure rather than a project. The Single Day (Advanced) template places one day against its week, month and year.
