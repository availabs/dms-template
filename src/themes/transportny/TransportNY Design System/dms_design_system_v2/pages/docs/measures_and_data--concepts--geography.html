Geography: regions, counties, MPOs, statewide.

What the geography words on the platform mean: NYSDOT's 11 regions, the 62 counties, the 14 metropolitan planning organizations and the urbanized areas. What a figure means when no geography is selected, and which pages let you choose which geography. Written for anyone who quotes a number.

The four geographies

"Statewide" on a TSMO dashboard is every segment in the dataset, deliberately not the sum of the 11 regions. The rest of this page explains that sentence and the four geographies around it.

NYSDOT region
One of the 11 administrative regions of the New York State Department of Transportation, written "Region 8". Region 1 is the Capital District and Region 11 is New York City. The eleven, by NYSDOT's regional offices: 1 Capital District (Albany), 2 Mohawk Valley (Utica), 3 Central New York (Syracuse), 4 Genesee Valley (Rochester), 5 Western New York (Buffalo), 6 Southern Tier Central (Hornell), 7 North Country (Watertown), 8 Hudson Valley (Poughkeepsie), 9 Southern Tier East (Binghamton), 10 Long Island (Hauppauge), 11 New York City. Every TSMO dashboard's Region filter rolls up to these.
County
One of New York's 62 counties, written "Albany County". The TMC network carries a county for each segment, so counties are the finest geography the operations pages key on.
Metropolitan planning organization (MPO)
One of the 14 federally designated planning bodies for New York's urbanized areas, written by acronym then name on first use. MPO boundaries do not follow region boundaries, so a segment can sit in one region and one MPO independently. New York's MPOs support the statewide MAP-21 targets rather than setting their own.
Urbanized area (UZA)
A Census-defined urban area. Peak Hour Excessive Delay (PHED) is reported per urbanized area over 200,000 people in an air-quality nonattainment or maintenance area. In New York only two report: New York–Newark and Poughkeepsie–Newburgh. Buffalo, Rochester, Albany–Schenectady and Syracuse exceed 200,000 people but are in attainment. Seven others are below the threshold.

Two other words often sit beside a geography and are not geographies. System is Interstate or non-Interstate National Highway System (NHS), the split the federal reliability figures use. Stream is all vehicles, passenger vehicles or freight trucks. State both when you quote a number.

What statewide means

On the TSMO dashboards, no region selected means statewide. The filter bar says so: no region selected = statewide. The trend chart's label reads statewide unless a region is selected. Clearing a region with its ✕ restores the statewide query.

Statewide on the Reliability dashboard is the full MAP-21 submission dataset, the same aggregate as the federal figures. It is intentionally not the sum of the 11 regions. The submission includes border and unassigned segments, rows with out-of-state county codes and rows with a blank county, and those segments belong to no region. Selecting every region one by one would leave them out. On the delay and event dashboards the same rule holds: statewide is every row, including segments assigned to no region.

The federal targets are statewide floors. A dense region can sit below the 75 % Interstate or 70 % non-Interstate reliability target while the state as a whole clears it. A region's figure is not scored against a regional target, because none exists.

On the Macro View, an empty geography box means the whole state. That is the right choice when you want the statewide distribution behind the legend. Adding a region, county or MPO narrows the map, the distribution summary, the worst-segments ranking and the download at once.

basisWhenever you quote a number, write the geography: statewide · Region 8 · Albany County · the MPO by name. "Statewide" is written, never implied by a blank.

Order, county view and MPO filtering

Region order. Region lists once sorted as text, so Region 10 and Region 11 followed Region 1. Since July 2026 every filter list on the platform sorts numerically, Region 2 before Region 10.

County view of the dashboards. A county filter for the TSMO statistics has been requested, for MPO use. The delay data carries a county code, so it is feasible, but it needs a county filter, county-grouped panels and a code-to-name lookup. It is scoped as a feature and is not built. Today, county-level operations data is in Corridor View and the county facet of Incident Search.

MPO filtering of the dashboards. MPOs asked for MPO geography in the dashboards' selector, and for a date. No MPO geography exists in the TSMO dashboards today. Adding it needs an MPO boundary set, a rule for segments that straddle a boundary, and a source the filters can bind to. No delivery date has been set. Until then, the two MPO geographies on the platform are the Macro View's MPO filter and the MAP-21 pages' per-MPO table.

The removed corridor drill. The Congestion dashboard once offered a "drill into a corridor" button. It was removed in July 2026. Corridor View is county-keyed, so the region selection could not carry across and the page landed on a default county. Open Corridor View from the side navigation or the corridor-explorer link instead, and choose the county yourself.

Where this matters

Regions do not sum to the state. Do not add them to get a statewide figure, and do not expect the statewide figure to match the sum. A region below a federal target is not out of compliance, because the target is statewide. A corridor is a county question. An MPO question is answered today on the Macro View or the MAP-21 pages, not on a TSMO dashboard. A region-month with no attributed events shows a cost of $0, which Coverage and freshness explains. Federal PM3 and what this site publishes explains why the statewide figure carries two bases.
