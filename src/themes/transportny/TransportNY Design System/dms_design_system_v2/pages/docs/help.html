Help and feedback.

How to report a problem, ask a question or request an account, and where to look before you do. One form takes every kind of report, and this page says what to put in it and what you will see afterwards.

reference applies to · all products last reviewed · 2026-09-08 owner · —

The form is the channel

Reports and questions go to the platform's issue form. Its address is /status/bug_report. The page reads TransportNY · Issue Tracker with the heading Submit an Issue, and the form writes a row to the tracker the team works from.

Nothing on the products links to it, so you reach it by typing the address. A link from the product pages is not promised. No telephone number or mailbox is published for the platform, and this documentation does not name one.

Three addresses make up the status pattern, which is mounted at /status:

AddressWhat it isUse it to
/status/bug_reportThe issue formReport a problem, ask a question, request an account or a file
/statusSite Status: an Alerts table over the tracker and a Data Sources bandCheck whether your problem is already known, and how fresh the data is
/status/issue_trackerThe whole tracker as one tableRead the tickets, including your own

Before you report

Four checks answer most reports, and each is a question the product FAQs answer in full.

  1. Has your session expired? Everything that reads is public, but saving needs an account and a session lasts about 6 hours, after which the site returns you to the sign-in page. At the 2026-09-08 check, signed-out visitors to the TSMO dashboards and the NPMRDS pages were sent to the sign-in page, whose own text says public dashboards need no account; that redirect is logged as an issue, and this page describes the intended access. See Sign in, sessions and accounts.A page that suddenly asks you to sign in, or a save that fails, is usually this.
  2. Which filters are set? A number that looks wrong is often a different year, region or vehicle class from the one you meant. See Using the filter bar and the platform FAQ.The page address carries the selection, so you can read it back.
  3. Does the page's own Limits section explain it? Every how-to and screens page states what its numbers do not cover, and the measure pages state their known limits. See Which measure should I use?
  4. Is it already known? Read the Alerts table at /status, and the FAQ for your product: NPMRDS, TSMO, the Freight Atlas.Those pages carry the answers to the questions asked most often, each linked to the page that should have pre-empted it.

If a number disagrees with a number you published elsewhere, read Federal PM3 and what this site publishes first. Two measure families share the same names, and that is the most common cause.

What the form asks for

The fields are below in the order the form carries them. None of them is described as required.

FieldTypeWhat to put in it
IDtextThe form's first field. This documentation does not say who fills it in.
DescriptiontextareaWhat you expected, what you saw instead, and the filters that were set. The long field, so use it.
URLtextThe address of the page you were on. Copy it from the browser: it carries the year, region and measure you had selected.
Date ReporteddateThe day you are filing. If the problem happened earlier, say when in the description.
Page / ComponenttextWhich page or panel it was, in the product's own words, such as the Congestion dashboard's delay composition chart.
StatusselectLeave it for the team unless you are updating a row you own.

A report that carries the address, the filters, the expected value, the value shown and the date can be reproduced without a reply. That is advice rather than the form's requirement.

What you will see afterwards

Your row appears in the Alerts table at /status and in the tracker at /status/issue_tracker, 25 rows to a page. Its Status is one of seven values, read plainly below.

StatusWhat it reads as on your ticket
New RequestFiled, and not yet sorted into the work
Queued ImmediateAccepted, and in the current run of work
Queued ScheduledAccepted, and held for a later run
Needs DiscussionOpen, and waiting on a decision or on more from you
Support NeededOpen, and waiting on someone outside the team
ResolvedDone
Not Recommended / RetiredClosed without the change, or no longer applicable
no datesA status is a label on a row. The tracker states no timings, and this documentation does not promise how long any status lasts or that a reply arrives.

Checking freshness and known issues

The /status page is titled Site Status and described as Data freshness, Alerts & Ticket Tracking. Its Alerts table is the same tracker the form writes to, so a problem someone else reported is visible there. Its Data Sources band carries two cards, NPMRDS Data and Transcom Events, which show how current each feed is.

For the fuller picture of what is loaded and how far behind it runs, use Coverage and freshness, which states the span, lag and cadence of every feed and the gaps in each series.

The feedback buttons on these pages

Every page of this documentation ends with Was this page helpful? and two buttons. They are not wired up yet: the note beside them says feedback lands in the ticket queue once live. Until then the issue form is the route for anything you want to tell us about a documentation page, and naming the page in Page / Component is enough for us to find it.

Requesting an account

Reading needs no account. Saving a route or a report, and administering data, do. An administrator adds an account by email address, and adding one grants nothing on its own: the groups on the account carry the permissions. See Users and permissions.

There is no approval queue in this build. The administrator's Users page lists accounts, not pending requests, and has no approve or reject action. So ask through the issue form, or ask an administrator in your own agency. Where a site has its own sign-up form enabled, that form sends the request to the authentication server rather than to a person, and the account still has to be given its groups. See Sign in, sessions and accounts.
