Data coverage.

How much of the travel-time feed actually arrived for a segment, as two percentages. Coverage describes the input, not the road, and it confounds every other measure. Read it first.

measure applies to · NPMRDS Macro View (www/npmrds) methodology 2026-08 (pm3 analytical series) last reviewed · 2026-09-04 owner · —

What it answers

How much of the underlying data actually arrived for this segment. Two percentages on the same 0-100 scale. Bins reporting is how much of a bin-based measure's own input arrived — the sample size its percentiles rest on. Epochs reporting is how much of the raw 5-minute feed arrived, and is lower by construction, since a bin counts as present when any one of its three epochs did.

basisFigures on this page are statewide, the full monitored network, with the stream, period and years named in each sentence.

How it is computed

  1. For the selected stream, all vehicles or freight trucks, and the selected period, count the 15-minute bins where that stream had a value. A bin where the feed had a row but the stream had no value does not count.
  2. Count the bins the period could hold in that year: its hours times its day-of-week mask. An AM peak holds 4,176 bins in 2025; an overnight period holds 14,600.
  3. Divide: bins present over bins possible. Repeat for 5-minute epochs to get epochs reporting.
coverage = present ÷ possible bins reporting = 15-minute bins with a value for the stream ÷ bins the period could hold in the year epochs reporting = 5-minute epochs present ÷ epochs the period could hold epochs reporting ÷ bins reporting × 3 = the mean number of epochs behind each bin, a measure of probe depth

Parameters and edge rules

  • Controls: stream (all vehicles or trucks) and peak period.
  • Published once per stream per bin, not once per measure. The all-vehicle AM-peak figure is LOTTR's sample; the overnight truck figure is the sample both TTTR and TTTR₈₀ rest on; the all-hours figure is what TED reads.
  • Denominators are per bin, derived from that bin's own hours and days, never shared.
  • The PM-peak ambiguity does not apply here. Coverage publishes 16:00–19:59 and 15:00–18:59 as separate bins, so either denominator is unambiguous.

The choices made, and why

  • Coverage is published as a measure in its own right, in its own menu group, so it cannot be mistaken for a property of traffic.
  • Two percentages, not one. Their ratio recovers probe depth, which is the term the sparsity bias actually tracks. Publishing only the bin figure would let a reader reason about sparsity with the sign inverted: removing bins deflates LOTTR, while thinning the probes behind each surviving bin inflates it, and the second effect dominates real sparsity.
  • The bin count is the count of bins where the stream had a value. Counting feed rows had inflated the truck figure roughly two times.

How much to trust it

Coverage is a count, not an estimate, so it carries no precision band. What matters is that it is not stationary. Daytime epoch coverage moves in nine abrupt, month-aligned eras between 2017 and 2026, from 28.0 % to 67.0 %, stepping down as often as up. Across the published years, all-vehicle bins reporting runs 36.3 % to 51.5 % and truck bins 8.0 % to 23.5 %.

Known limits

  • Thin data biases the two measure families in opposite directions. It inflates the reliability ratios and deflates the delay measures. They will disagree under a coverage change rather than both drifting one way.
  • 88.1 % of records rest on four probes or fewer, and trucks are absent from 59.6 % of records outright.
  • Coverage counts presence, not quality. A bin with one probe counts the same as a bin with forty. Use the epochs-to-bins ratio for depth.

Comparability across years and versions

Compare within a coverage era, or state that you are crossing one. Every published row carries an era tag for each stream and a flag when the year crosses a boundary. Use the tag that matches the measure's stream: all vehicles for LOTTR, PHED and TED; trucks for TTTR and TTTR₈₀. See Coverage eras and comparability.
