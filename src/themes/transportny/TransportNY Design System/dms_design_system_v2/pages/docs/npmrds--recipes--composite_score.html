Recipe: a composite bottleneck score.

Rank every segment in a county or region by several measures at once. Export one PM3 year from the Macro View, screen out thin data, normalise each measure to a 1 to 5 scale, weight them and rank the sum in a spreadsheet. Rewritten from the 2024 Composite Score Tutorial for the current download builder. Assumes you can use Excel tables and structured references.

recipe applies to · NPMRDS last reviewed · 2026-09-07 owner · —

The question

"Which segments in my region are the worst bottlenecks, taking reliability and delay together?" One measure answers one question. LOTTR finds the segments whose bad days are far worse than their typical days. TED finds the segments that lose the most vehicle-hours. A ranking by either alone will surprise you: a short ramp tops the reliability list, a long freeway tops the delay list. A composite score puts the measures on one scale, lets you say how much each matters, and ranks the sum.

What you need

  • The Macro View and its download builder. No sign-in.
  • A spreadsheet that supports tables and MIN, MAX and RANK. The formulas below are Excel's, with structured references such as [@[lottr_amp_lottr]].
  • A geography and a PM3 year. This recipe uses Albany County and the latest published year; the 2024 tutorial used Albany County and 2019.
  • Segment lengths, if you want delay per mile. The Macro View dataset carries no length, so you join it from the TMC network table or read it from the GeoPackage's geometry in GIS.
basisOne PM3 year · one geography from the Geography control · all vehicles for LOTTR and TED, freight trucks for TTTR₈₀ · every monitored segment in the geography · the analytical series, not the federal figures.

Steps

  1. Open Macro View. Under Geography, type the county and pick it under Counties. Pick the Year. Set Measure to LOTTR and Peak period to PM Peak.The map, the distribution and the row count in Download N rows narrow to the county.
  2. Select Download N rows. Keep Scope on Current filters and set Format to csv.
  3. Under Columns you'll get, the PM-peak LOTTR column is already listed with the identity columns. Use Add measure column to add the other measures you will score and the coverage columns you will screen on: the AM and midday LOTTR ratios, TTTR₈₀ for the PM peak, TED against the fixed free-flow reference in vehicle-hours (ted_freeflow_anchored_all_xdelay_vhrs), and the share of bins reporting for the AM, midday and PM periods (coverage_all_vehicles_amp_pct_bins_reporting and its midday and PM counterparts). Coverage columns begin with coverage_ and name the stream and period; the truck 80th-percentile ratio is the tttr_p80 column with its period suffix. Read the exact names from the download's header row.
  4. Select Build N rows · csv, wait for Download N rows · csv, and save the zip. Open PM3_README.txt and note the coverage era line; you will quote it.
  5. Open the CSV, select all, and insert a table with headers. Move the identity columns (tmc, county, region, MPO) to the left if you want; the formulas use column names, so order does not matter.
  6. Screen for data quality. Add a column Above Threshold with =MAX([@[coverage_all_vehicles_amp_pct_bins_reporting]],[@[…midd…]],[@[…pmp…]])>30. Coverage is on a 0 to 100 scale, so the 2024 tutorial's >0.3 becomes >30. Filter to FALSE, delete those table rows, then clear the filter.Every remaining segment had at least 30 % of its 15-minute bins reporting in at least one daytime period.
  7. If you want delay per mile, add a miles column joined from the TMC network on tmc, then ted_per_mi as =[@[ted_freeflow_anchored_all_xdelay_vhrs]]/[@miles]. The download already carries miles among its segment attributes, so no separate network export is needed.
  8. Normalise each measure to 1 to 5. For each measure column add a NORM column:
    =1+4*(([@[lottr_pmp_lottr]])-MIN([lottr_pmp_lottr]))/(MAX([lottr_pmp_lottr])-MIN([lottr_pmp_lottr]))The lowest value in the column becomes 1, the highest 5, the rest in proportion. Repeat for TTTR₈₀ and for TED or TED per mile.
  9. Rank each measure with =RANK([@[lottr_pmp_lottr]],[lottr_pmp_lottr],0). The trailing 0 ranks descending, so rank 1 is the worst segment on that measure.
  10. Add Composite Score as the weighted sum of the normalised columns, for example =([@lottrNORM]*0.5)+([@tttr80NORM]*0.25)+([@tedNORM]*0.25). Change the weights to state what matters to your process. The 2024 tutorial weighted three measures at 0.5 each.
  11. Add Composite Rank as =RANK([@[Composite Score]],[Composite Score],0) and sort the table by it, smallest first.Row 1 is the segment that scores worst across the measures you chose, at the weights you chose.

Reading the result

The rank is only as good as its inputs, so read three things before you act on it. Length. 24.3 % of segments are under 0.05 miles, and those are flagged unreliable 7.7 times more often than long ones. Keep the miles column visible and treat a very short segment at the top as a signal about its neighbours, not itself. Which delay variant. The anchored free-flow TED is the default because its yardstick does not move between years; for a ranking that crosses functional classes, add the relative (unfloored) variant instead, because a floored figure on an arterial is about 90 % floor. Which measure should I use? has the table. Coverage. The 30 % screen removes the thinnest segments, but a segment at 31 % is still thin. Add the coverage column to the sheet and glance at it for the top rows.

A composite score is a ranking device, not a measure. Report the individual measures for each top segment, and say the weights. Two analysts with different weights will produce different top tens from the same file, and both are right about their own question.

Variations

  • Peak hours only. Replace TED with PHED, whose window is the weekday AM and PM peaks, in person-hours. Note that PHED's PM peak is 15:00 to 18:59 while LOTTR's is 16:00 to 19:59; keep them in separate columns.
  • Freight. Score TTTR₈₀ alone, or add the truck TED column (Traffic type · All Trucks when you add the column). Truck coverage runs 8 % to 23.5 % of bins by era, so the screen matters more.
  • A region or an MPO. Pick it under Regions or MPOs instead of a county. The download's row count tells you the size before you build.
  • Two years. Export each year separately, keep both READMEs, and compare ranks rather than values. The era tags and the AADT vintage move between years; Coverage eras and comparability says when a comparison holds.
  • The 2024 measure set. The tutorial used LOTTR, the Travel Time Index (TTI) and TED per mile. TTI is not currently computed; Measures that used to be here names the substitutes.
