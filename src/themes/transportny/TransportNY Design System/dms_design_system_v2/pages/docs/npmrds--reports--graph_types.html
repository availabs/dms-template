Graph types reference.

The five shapes the Add Graph dialog offers, the two comparison modes, the seven resolutions and the eleven measures, each with what it shows and how it reads. A table maps the graph names of the 2024 documentation to today's shapes.

The five shapes

A graph card is one question about one or more routes: a shape, a measure, a resolution, a time-of-day and day-of-week window, and for charts a comparison mode. Every route on the card keeps its identity colour, so a reader learns the key once. The shape decides how the answer is drawn.

Bar Graph

One bar per time bucket, good for comparing values across routes at a glance. At Day resolution across a year it is the place to spot event days: a single tall bar of hours of delay is an incident or weather, a run of them is a work zone or a holiday pattern. At Summary (one bar per route) it collapses each route to one value, which is how the templates' AM, PM and off-peak comparisons are drawn.

Line Graph

A continuous line per route, good for trends. At 5 Minutes or Hour across a day it draws the daily profile: the morning and evening peaks in travel time, or the dips in speed. Put the same route in twice with two windows and the two lines are a before-and-after overlay. It is the default shape.

Grid Graph

The route's segments run top to bottom in route order and time runs left to right; each cell is the measure's value for one segment in one bucket. The 2024 tool drew each row's height in proportion to the segment's length and marked the direction of travel with an arrow; the live grid's row sizing is a rendering detail, not a reading. Read a grid for shape: a dark vertical band is a time when the whole route slowed; a dark horizontal band is a segment that is slow all day; a wedge spreading downward over time is a queue growing back from a bottleneck. In Difference mode the colour is one route minus the other, so a diverging scale is used and the middle colour means no change.

Table

The same measure and resolution as a chart, in rows and columns. It is the only shape that takes several measures at once, one column each, from the Measures (pick any) checklist. At Summary (one row per route) two extras become available: Route Compare, which adds a percent-against-the-first-route column per measure, and Reliability, which adds LOTTR, TTTR and free-flow speed columns when the card's When window is exactly a federal peak period or the weekend and the routes' year has published values. Those columns are the platform's analytical series, not the federal submittal; see Federal PM3 and what this site publishes.

Map

The selected routes drawn on a map. Color by is None — just show the route or one of speed, travel time, hours of delay, average hours of delay and the four emissions measures, each with fixed colour breaks so the legend does not move between reports. A Map has no resolution and no time-of-day window at creation; its colour is the measure over the route's whole date window.

Comparison mode: Plain and Difference

Plain overlays every route on the card; the pill reads Overlay. Difference subtracts one route from the other and needs exactly two routes; the pill reads Diff. The route named under Anchor Route is the one subtracted from, so the value is anchor minus other: on a travel-time card with the "before" period as anchor, a positive bar means before was slower, that is, things improved. The dialog offers the anchor choice only once two routes are ticked, and the default anchor is whichever of the two sits first in the report's route list. Maps and Tables have no comparison mode; a Table compares through its Route Compare column instead.

Measures offered

The measures in the Measure list, in the dialog's groups. Every value is computed for the route as a whole from all-vehicle travel times, except the truck speed and the truck emissions.

The 2024 documentation also listed Data Quality, Freeflow, Buffer Time Index, Planning Time Index, Misery Index, Travel Time Index and the 95th and 97th percentile travel times as report measures. None is in today's Measure list. Free-flow speed and the reliability ratios appear as a Table's Reliability columns; coverage and percentile speed are Macro View measures. Definitions live on the measure pages, which take precedence over the 2024 text.

Where this matters

Choose the shape by the question: a profile wants a line, event days want daily bars, "where on the route" wants a grid, exact values want a table, and the map is for the reader who does not know the road. Make the comparison explicit with Difference only when two windows of one route are on the card and you have named the anchor.
