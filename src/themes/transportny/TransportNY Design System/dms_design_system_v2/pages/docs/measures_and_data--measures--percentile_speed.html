Percentile speed.

The speed a chosen share of trips beat, per segment and period. This page defines how speed is derived from travel time and where the derivation breaks. It assumes you know what a TMC segment is.

What it answers

What speed a chosen share of trips beat — the floating-car view. The chosen percentile of observed speed over all travel times.

Percentile speed is not a federal measure. It is the underlying distribution the reliability ratios summarise, shown directly.

basisFigures on this page are CY 2025, statewide, all vehicles, the full monitored network, unless stated.

How it is computed

  1. Take the segment's all-vehicle travel times for the year, averaged into 15-minute bins, for the selected period.
  2. Convert each bin's travel time to a speed: segment length in miles divided by travel time in seconds, times 3,600.
  3. Take the chosen percentile of those speeds. Eight percentiles are published: 5th, 20th, 25th, 50th, 75th, 80th, 85th and 95th.
speed = miles ÷ travel_time × 3600 percentile speed = quantile(speed, p) miles = the segment's length · travel_time = the bin's travel time in seconds · 3600 converts to miles per hour quantile(speed, p) = the value that a share p of bins fall below · p ∈ {5, 20, 25, 50, 75, 80, 85, 95} per segment × period × calendar year · all-vehicle stream · no imputation for missing bins

Parameters and edge rules

  • Controls: percentile and peak period. All periods are available.
  • Stream: all vehicles.
  • Missing bins: the percentile is taken over the bins that exist. Nothing is imputed.
  • No threshold. A speed has no federal pass or fail.

The choices made, and why

  • Speed is derived, not measured. The feed carries travel time. Speed is segment length divided by it, so segment length enters every speed figure.
  • Sub-second travel times are kept. 8,292,469 CY 2025 records (0.489 %) have a travel time under one second. They occur only on segments shorter than 0.05 miles. A 53-foot segment at 38.5 mph legitimately takes 0.94 seconds. Removing them would empty one segment and leave 136 with under 10 % of their data, to move the per-segment reference by +0.009 %.
  • No impossibility screen. One was specified and then dropped, because the feed arrives already clamped and no impossible-value population is left to remove.

How much to trust it

No precision band is published. The percentile depth is the thing to watch: the 5th and 95th read a thin tail and are far less stable than the median on a sparse segment. Check Data coverage for the segment before reading a tail percentile.

Worked example

Feed bounds, CY 2025, statewide: zero of 1,695,358,623 records fall below 2 mph, and the maximum implied speed is 101.7 mph, with only 99 records above 100 mph.

Known limits

  • Bad geometry produces impossible speeds. On very short segments a one-second travel-time error becomes a large speed error. Any percentile pooled across segments of different lengths carries this artifact: pooled statewide, the same sub-second removal looks like +2.75 % instead of +0.009 %.
  • The feed contains no records below 2 mph, a vendor floor we cannot inspect. Very low speeds are truncated before we see them.
  • 36.2 % of records show trucks moving faster than passenger cars, which is implausible at scale and is a property of the feed, not the road.

Comparability across years and versions

Thinning the probes behind a bin changes which trips are observed, so a tail percentile moves with coverage. Compare within a coverage era, or say that you are crossing one. Segment lengths also change with the network vintage, and length enters every speed. See The TMC network and its vintages.
