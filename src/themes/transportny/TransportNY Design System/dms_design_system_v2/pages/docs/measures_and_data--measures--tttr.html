Truck Travel Time Reliability (TTTR).

The freight version of reliability, read at the 95th percentile. This page defines the per-segment analytical series and explains why its precision bar is unreachable. It assumes you have read the LOTTR page.

What it answers

The same question for freight, at a stricter percentile. The 95th-percentile truck travel time divided by the 50th, per segment per peak period.

TTTR is a federal measure under 23 CFR 490 subpart F. The federal figure is an Interstate index, not a per-segment value: the length-weighted mean of each segment's worst period. The Macro View shows the per-segment ratio for one period. Both come from the same feed through different pipelines. See Federal PM3 and what this site publishes.

basisFigures on this page are CY 2025, statewide, freight trucks with an all-vehicle fallback, the full monitored network, unless stated. The federal index is Interstate only.

How it is computed

  1. Take the segment's freight-truck travel times for the calendar year, one per 5-minute epoch.
  2. Where a 15-minute bin has no truck value, substitute the all-vehicle travel time for that bin.
  3. Average into 15-minute bins and assign each to a period: AM 06:00–09:59, midday 10:00–15:59, PM 16:00–19:59, weekend 06:00–19:59, overnight 20:00–05:59.
  4. Within each period, take the 95th-percentile and 50th-percentile bin travel time and divide.
  5. For the federal index only: take each Interstate segment's worst period, weight by segment length, and average statewide.
TTTR = TT₉₅ ÷ TT₅₀ TT₉₅ = the 95th-percentile truck travel time of the segment's 15-minute bins in the period TT₅₀ = the 50th-percentile (median) truck travel time of the same bins federal index = Σ (length × worst-period TTTR) ÷ Σ length, over Interstate segments

The choices made, and why

  • The 95/50 pair is kept unchanged. It is the federal definition.
  • Where a bin has no truck data, an all-vehicle travel time is substituted. This is a real choice with a measured cost. About 37 % of AM-peak bins carry a substituted value. Removing the substitution moves TTTR by +1.06 % (1.8821 to 1.9020 over 400 segments). Defensible, but TTTR is not a pure truck measure.
  • Reading the 95th percentile of a thin stream is what makes this measure expensive. That cost is the entire reason TTTR₈₀ exists alongside it. At matched sample size the truck stream is the easier of the two; the cost is the quantile.
  • Density weighting is retained for trucks only. For the all-vehicle stream it was no better than a plain count.

How much to trust it

A per-segment TTTR never meets its own precision bar. ±0.05 precision on 90 % of segments needs 57,832 overnight bins. An overnight year contains 14,600. The bar is unreachable by construction, and only 1.15 % of CY 2025 segments clear it in any period. That is the honest signal, not a typo. The measured spread at 25 bins is 0.304, at 250 bins 0.096, at 2,000 bins 0.027.

The federal index is a large aggregate and is far more stable. The FHWA HPMS Travel Time Metrics resubmission for CY 2025 gives a statewide Interstate TTTR of 1.46, against the 2.00 target. The MAP-21 pages compute the same 1.46 on the platform. Both are the federal measure. The per-segment analytical ratio is a different quantity and is not comparable to either.

Known limits

  • Truck coverage is thin and has changed enormously. Truck reporting bins run from 8.0 % to 23.5 % across the coverage eras. A year-over-year truck comparison is a coverage comparison unless checked.
  • Overnight is the thinnest data on the network. Treat a single-segment overnight TTTR as indicative only.
  • Trucks are absent from 59.6 % of records outright, so the fallback is engaged often.
  • For per-segment or corridor work, prefer TTTR₈₀. Reserve TTTR for comparison against federal figures and for large aggregates.

Comparability across years and versions

The truck stream has its own era history, on different dates from the all-vehicle stream. Truck coverage steps up at 2021-01, 2023-06 and 2026-02. At the 2023-06 boundary the two streams moved in opposite directions. Use the truck era tag for TTTR, never the all-vehicle one. See Coverage eras and comparability.

Build a Macro View download with TTTR selected. The package carries the ratio per period, the bin count, the precision band, the 57,832-bin bar and the truck era tag. The federal index comes from the MAP-21 annual download. See Download data from the Macro View.
