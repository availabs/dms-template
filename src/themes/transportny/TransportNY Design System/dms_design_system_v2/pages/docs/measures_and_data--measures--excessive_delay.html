Excessive delay.

Vehicle-hours spent below a congestion threshold on New York's monitored roads, per segment and month. This is the canonical definition behind the TSMO Congestion dashboard and the TSMO home figures. It assumes you know what a TMC segment and AADT are.

What it answers

How much time traffic loses to congestion, where, and in which month. The TSMO Data & Methodology page defines it as: "Time spent traveling slower than the excessive-delay threshold — the greater of 20 mph or 60% of the posted speed — multiplied by the vehicles present in that 5-minute slice. Summed to TMC × month, then split into cause buckets by overlapping TRANSCOM events."

Excessive delay is the platform's operational delay series. It is not the federal PHED and not the Macro View's TED, although all three use the same threshold formula. The differences are listed under Comparability.

basisFigures on this page are CY 2025, statewide, all vehicles, the monitored network with a posted speed limit and an AADT, unless stated.

How it is computed

  1. Set the segment's threshold speed: the greater of 20 mph and 60 % of the posted speed limit. Convert to a threshold travel time: length divided by threshold speed.
  2. For each 5-minute slice, take the observed travel time minus the threshold travel time, floored at zero.
  3. Multiply by the vehicles present in the slice: the segment's directional AADT times the slice's share of daily traffic, from a fixed daily traffic profile for the road type and day type.
  4. Sum the slices to segment × month. This is total excessive delay, in vehicle-hours.
  5. Repeat step 2 against the greater of the threshold and the segment's recurrent baseline, the median weekday travel time for that slice over the year. This is non-recurrent delay. Recurrent delay is total minus non-recurrent.
  6. Split non-recurrent delay into construction, accident and other by the TRANSCOM events that overlap the segment and month. See Delay attribution.
threshold = max(20 mph, 0.6 × posted) delay_5min = max(0, TT_obs − TT_thr) × veh_5min veh-hrs = Σ delay_5min per TMC × month recurrent = total − non-recurrent posted = the posted speed limit · TT_obs = observed travel time · TT_thr = threshold travel time (length ÷ threshold speed) veh_5min = vehicles counted in that 5-minute slice, from directional AADT × the slice's share of the day · delay_5min = delay in that slice veh-hrs = vehicle-hours · Σ = the sum over every slice in the month · non-recurrent uses max(TT_thr, baseline travel time) in place of TT_thr

Parameters and edge rules

  • Floor: 20 mph. Reference: the posted speed limit, so the threshold measures degradation from a policy number, not from what the road can achieve.
  • Time slice: 5 minutes, not the 15-minute bins of the Macro View measures.
  • Baseline: the median weekday travel time per segment and slice over the year. The earlier series used the mean.
  • Volume: AADT split by direction, weighted across the day by a fixed national profile. A segment needs a posted speed limit and an AADT to carry delay.
  • Unit: vehicle-hours. Person-hours are vehicle-hours times an occupancy; dollars are a separate step, see Cost of congestion.
  • Geography: statewide, or one of the 11 NYSDOT regions from the Region filter. See Geography.
  • Partial years show partial totals. A year with four months of data shows four months of delay, presented in the same card as a full year.

The choices made, and why

  • The posted-limit threshold is now real. In the earlier series the speed-limit field was empty for every year, so the formula collapsed to a uniform 20 mph threshold statewide. We backfilled posted limits from the network geometry in June 2026, and the current series applies the greater of 20 mph and 60 % of the limit.
  • The baseline is the median, not the mean. A yearly mean over all days includes the disrupted days themselves. Big events depressed their own baseline, undercounting non-recurrent delay, and a year with many incidents looked "more recurrent".
  • Attribution is capped. Two events on the same segment and time used to each claim the full excess. Bucket sums now cannot exceed the segment-month's non-recurrent delay.
  • The series was restarted, not patched. Because the new threshold and baseline are not comparable with the old, the recomputation started in a new series, filling from the newest complete month backwards. The old series stays frozen.
  • Vehicle-hours stay the stored unit. Monetisation and occupancy are conversions stated once, not baked into the measure.

How much to trust it

No precision band is published. Three things bound the figure. The daily traffic profile that spreads AADT over the day is a static national profile, not a New York count, so the within-day weighting is approximate. Missing probe slices contribute no delay, so a thinly observed month reads low. And the series covers only segments with both a posted limit and an AADT. The recurrent and non-recurrent split is robust to single events because the baseline is a median.

Worked example

CY 2025, statewide, all vehicles, monitored network, as measured for the Congestion dashboard's data revision of 2026-06-13: total excessive delay 310.86 million vehicle-hours, of which 157.07 million (51 %) non-recurrent. Construction events account for 46.84 million and accidents for 4.82 million. By region, Region 11 carries 219.5 million vehicle-hours with 42 % non-recurrent, Region 8 carries 22.9 million with 73 % non-recurrent, and Region 6 carries 0.3 million with 95 % non-recurrent. Upstate delay is overwhelmingly event-driven; New York City's is recurrent.

The live dashboard recomputes as the backfill lands; the figures above are the June 2026 revision's.

Known limits

  • Not every year is computed. As of the Data & Methodology page's July 2026 revision, the current series covers 2018 and 2021–2025, with 2019 and 2020 shown as an explicit gap. The backfill continues; the Data & Methodology page's refresh-cadence table shows the latest month loaded (through October 2025 at its last update).
  • Partial years look like full ones. The Year filter offers every year with any data. A month with no data shows nothing, not zero.
  • Not comparable with earlier published figures. Numbers from before the June 2026 revision used a flat 20 mph threshold and a mean baseline. Never mix them.
  • Roads that cannot reach their posted limit report delay in normal operation. This is a property of the posted-limit reference.
  • Delay is linear in AADT, which has its own revision cycle. See TED, known limits.

Comparability across years and versions

Within the current series, compare complete years to complete years and read the region filter as a filter on segments. Do not compare this series to PHED or TED: they use 15-minute bins, an annual scope, and, by default, a free-flow reference rather than the posted limit. Do not compare it to numbers published before June 2026. A dated list of changes is in Methodology changes.
