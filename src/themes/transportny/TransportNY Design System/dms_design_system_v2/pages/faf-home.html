Freight Analysis Framework.

FAF5.7.1132 FAF zones42 commodities8 modesbase year 2017forecast 2050

FAF is the only public source that estimates freight movement for every mode, every commodity and every origin–destination pair in the country at once — a benchmark year built on the Commodity Flow Survey, annual estimates on top of it, and a forecast to 2050. It is a modelled synthesis, not a count of shipments, and this family of pages is built to keep that distinction visible while you use it.

This page says New York moved 729.8M tons in 2021. The Freight Atlas says 936.5M. Both are right.

The two surfaces count different universes. The Atlas headline comes from S&P Global TRANSEARCH via the 2024 State Freight Plan and includes through movements — freight that neither originates nor terminates in New York, which the plan puts at about 38% of state tonnage. FAF's regional database has no through-movement concept at all: every row is an origin–destination pair, so a truck crossing the state on I-90 between Ohio and Massachusetts appears in neither zone's total. Use FAF for national comparability and commodity detail; use TRANSEARCH for what physically touches the state's roads. the Atlas front door.

Why “NY-involved” and not “New York”

A FAF row belongs to New York if either endpoint is a New York zone. Summing that across all 50 states double-counts every interstate flow, so state shares of the national total do not add to 100%. It is still the right basis for “what moves on New York's system,” and the wrong one for “what New York produces or consumes” — for that, the state page publishes a true-endpoint decomposition instead.

What FHWA says FAF is good for
verbatim from the July 2022 FHWA webinar
  • Publicly available, and the only public source covering every mode nationally
  • One consistent frame for 132 zones, 42 commodities, 8 modes, 13 year-columns
  • Carries international trade, not just domestic shipments
  • Supports network flow analysis on the National Highway System
  • Balanced forecast assumptions, with published high and low scenarios
And what it says FAF is not
the same slide’s limitations list
  • Not tailored to any specific region, and may conflict with local growth scenarios
  • Local roadways are not fully captured
  • May not have enough granularity for local-scale analysis, which likely needs supplemental data
  • Commodity detail may be insufficient for some analyses
The county-level technical report is blunter, quoting a 2013 FHWA workshop: FAF “cannot be used to address concerns at a level of granularity more detailed than annual statewide flows.”

Which release, and why it matters

The documents in this workspace describe FAF5.0 and FAF5.2; the data file is FAF5.7.1. The 2017 benchmark and the 2050 baseline are identical across those releases — verified column by column against the domestic modal table FHWA published for FAF5.2 — but the 2018–2024 annual estimates are release-specific and will move. Cite the release number with the figure.
