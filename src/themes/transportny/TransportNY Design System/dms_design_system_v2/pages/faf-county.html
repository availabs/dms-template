// 01executive summary

What the county product is, and what it is not.

This is a disaggregation. BTS took FAF5.6.1's 2022 zone-to-zone tonnage and split it to counties using three independent allocation methods — proportional allocation on employment and population, a re-estimated Cambridge Systematics regression, and an ORNL production/attraction procedure with matrix balancing — then built separate validation targets for water, rail and truck and combined the three with an ordinary least-squares composite. The result adds up: the four released tables sum to 19,629,054 thousand tons against FAF5.7.1's own 2022 national total excluding modes 7 and 8 of 19,627,916, and New York's marginals reconcile to the zone figures essentially exactly. Adding up is not the same as being right, and BTS says so directly: the estimates “do not benefit from nationwide ground truth measures of tonnage by commodity for all modes,” and validating them “defies traditional methods of calculating estimation variability.”

key findings
  • Erie is the largest county at 96.6M tons — ahead of New York County's 79.4M
  • The five largest counties are 50.0% of state tonnage
  • Truck and air are merged into one code; modes 7 and 8 are dropped
  • 42 commodities become 5 groups
  • Built on FAF5.6.1, so it is not consistent with the current annual estimates

BTS's own list of what is weak in it.

Water validation uses 2021 principal-port volumes against otherwise-2022 data. The rail validation rests on the Public Use Waybill, which has “major limitations, including significant suppression of flows” — the Confidential Waybill “would likely provide better information.” Pipeline estimates use only network constraints. Multimodal estimates assume the flow pattern is essentially the same as truck at origins and destinations. And the employment data underneath the allocation is itself suppressed below three establishments, which “could lead to an underestimation of the number of employees in specific industries for some counties.”

It is built on FAF5.6.1, so it will not tie to the current annual estimates.

The county release used FAF5.6.1 as its input; the regional file on the rest of this site is FAF5.7.1, and the 2018–2024 annual estimates were revised between them. The national totals still agree to 0.006% because the revisions are small in aggregate, but a county figure and a zone figure from different releases are not the same vintage and should not be differenced.

And no value, which rules out a whole class of question

tons_2022 is the only measure. New York's state-wide 2022 average was $1,796 a ton — useful for scale and wrong as a multiplier, because county commodity mixes differ by two orders of magnitude in value density. A county shipping gravel at $9 a ton and a county shipping pharmaceuticals at $66,479 cannot share an average.
