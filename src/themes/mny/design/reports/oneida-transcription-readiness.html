The short answer

A well-formed plan against a template it was not written for

The package is unusually clean. All 46 annexes run the same instrument — 548 tables, and after one classifier fix zero of them failed to classify. Every annex rates the same ten hazards, carries exactly two fully-specified action worksheets, and lays out its narrative under the same labels. That uniformity is what makes a 46-annex load a batch job rather than 46 separate readings.

All 46 annexes resolve to distinct Jurisdictions geoids with no collisions, and every Jurisdictions lexical column for Oneida is currently empty — so the narrative load has nothing to overwrite and no merge to negotiate.

The mismatch is the hazard taxonomy, and it is structural. Oneida’s risk assessment is a modified HAZNY — New York State’s own instrument, which MitigateNY 2.0 never names. It profiles 32 hazards, of which 20 are non-natural and share a single Local Context box in the 2.0 template. Of MitigateNY’s 16 natural-hazard pages, 11 have a real Oneida source, 3 need a judgement call and 2 — Hail and Lightning — have nothing at all.

At jurisdiction level it is sharper still: the annex grid rates ten hazards, and five of those ten have no MitigateNY type.

Actions are ahead of the plan, and carry a defect. 196 of the workbook’s 226 actions are already live. But 21 are missing across five jurisdictions, 9 more are labelled for a jurisdiction that does not exist, and 15 rows belonging to Wayne, Wyoming and Fulton counties are currently filed under Oneida geoids.

The 92 annex action worksheets are the package’s most valuable unexploited asset: they carry alternatives, benefits, useful life and timeframes that are empty on every row of the workbook.

Phase 0 — what kind of source is this?

The transcription skill makes this the first question because it decides whether the work is hours or weeks. Oneida does not match any of the four consultant profiles on file.

Author Oneida County Soil & Water Conservation District, in house. Not a consultant plan — no Tetra Tech, Hagerty or IEM fingerprints. This is a new document class and wants its own Layer 2 profile.
Shape Mixed, leaning prose. 519,258 characters of authored annex narrative under stable labels, plus a tight fixed instrument of 11 tables per annex. Neither the survey-shaped Tetra Tech playbook nor the prose-shaped 1.0-site playbook transfers cleanly.
Already delivered? Yes, and largely already loaded. The action updates workbook is the DHSES intake instrument — its header row is the Actions schema and its Validation sheet is the live select vocabulary. Verified by count, not assumed.
Taxonomy match? No — and it under- and over-runs at once. HAZNY over-runs MitigateNY with 20 non-natural hazards, and under-runs it by omitting Hail and Lightning entirely.
Checkboxes? None. Probed the raw XML of all 46 annexes: zero w14:checkbox content controls and zero text boxes. The python-docx checkbox trap that cost the Suffolk run does not apply here.
The trap this corpus sets

Heading styles cannot carry the spine

Heading-styled paragraphs per annex range from 1 to 20. Six annexes have three or fewer. An extractor that walks the heading tree — which is what worked on every previous county — would silently produce near-empty output for a third of the corpus and give no indication it had.

The spine is carried instead by table shape plus normalised header text, with the narrative split on the annexes’ own short unstyled label paragraphs.

Header text drifts, and the failure is silent. The first classifier pass left 9 tables unclassified across the corpus, all from wording drift: Local Regulation singular in 3 annexes, Adaptive Capacity or a double-spaced Adaptive  Capability in 5, and one scoring table whose caption row is the project name. Word’s repeat-header-row setting also emits row 0 twice in several files. After the fix: 0 unclassified out of 548.

The plan contradicts itself

The list under Hazards within Oneida County Plan names 33 hazards; the Hazard Descriptions chapter profiles 32. Once wording variants are folded, two hazards are declared but never profiled — Structural Collapse and Terrorism — and two are profiled but never declared — Food Shortage and Oil Spill.

There is no basis in the document for deciding whether Structural Collapse or Terrorism is a hazard of concern. Ask the county; do not infer.

21 actions are missing outright

Five jurisdictions hold zero live actions despite having workbook rows and their own annex: New Hartford (Town) 8, Sherrill (City) 5, Oneida Castle (Village) 4, Oneida County itself 3, and Ava 1.

They are not a taxonomy problem or a matching artefact — the rows simply are not there. This is the clearest piece of load work in the whole package.

Two labels that cannot be resolved

Vienna (Village) — 9 actions. There is no village of Vienna in Oneida County. The annex is 8.40 Vienna (T), geoid 3606577486, which currently holds zero actions. Almost certainly the Town, but that is the county’s call.

Camden (Town/Village) — 6 actions under one label for two jurisdictions with separate annexes. All six resolved to the Town; the Village holds zero.

Utica has two Jurisdictions rows — 3676540 (City / Place) and 3606576540 (city / cousub). Its 18 actions are filed under the second. Worth resolving before the annex narrative lands on the first.

The 92 annex action worksheets — the package’s best unused content

Every annex carries exactly two fully-specified FEMA action worksheets (a 26×9 detail table and a 20×3 scoring table), confirmed by probing the raw XML of all 46 files: 92 worksheets, no more hiding in text boxes.

They matter because they fill columns the workbook leaves empty on every one of its 226 rows:

The catch is coverage: 92 worksheets against 226 workbook actions. They are a curated top-two per jurisdiction, so they enrich about 41% of the action set and no more.

The scoring tables cannot be reconciled at all

Each worksheet is paired with a prioritisation table scoring 14 criteria 0 or 1 with a written justification for each: Life Safety, Property Protection, Cost-Effectiveness, Technical, Political, Legal, Fiscal, Environmental, Social, Administrative, Multi-Hazard, Timeline, Agency Champion, Other Community Objectives. That is 92 tables and about 1,288 individual scored judgements, each with prose.

The live Actions schema carries a different five-criterion instrument scored 0–4 — Probability of Acceptance, Funding Availability, Probability of Matching Funds, Benefit Cost Review, Environmental Benefit — all five marked required, and all five empty on every one of the 226 workbook rows.

The two instruments share no criterion and no scale. There is no arithmetic that turns one into the other, and a crosswalk would be invention. Either the scores stay out of the system, or MitigateNY gains a column that can hold a local prioritisation instrument on its own terms — which is the author-empowerment answer, and would serve every county that scores its actions its own way.
