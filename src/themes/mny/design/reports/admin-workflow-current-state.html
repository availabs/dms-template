The verdict

The workflow moved into the county sites.
The admin panel stayed behind.

Three counties are actively writing plans today — Suffolk (163 annotation slots drafted), Schenectady (55), Westchester (54) — and every bit of that work happens inside their own county site: filling the light-blue Annotation slots the template marks for them, reading the Inline Guidance next to each one, and rendering jurisdictional annexes through a single page driven by ?geoid_juris= URL state. The template even carries its own hidden data-entry workbench now — Actions Edit List, Action View/Edit, a Hazards-of-Concern form prototype, a critical-infrastructure intake form — all built in plan context, plus the new /actions pattern with its jurisdiction-picker → workspace flow.

The admin panel predates all of it. It has grown to 112 pages by cloning variants (create_full / create_simple / create_new, two "(old)" trees, two "Dup" roots) without a cleanup pass; a third of it is duplicate, legacy, or archived; its most-maintained surfaces duplicate pages the county template already has. The only thing that still routes a planner into it — the "Add Data" cards on plan pages and two guide pages — is mostly broken: 7 of the 10 "Add Data" rows point at admin pages that no longer exist.

None of this means the panel's job disappeared. Six forms datasets are genuinely county-entered, required, and rendered throughout every plan. The question the two companion reports take up is where that job should live — in a cleaned-up panel, or inside the plan the way actions already went.

How a planner actually moves through the platform

Three surfaces exist. Only one of them is where planners live. The arrows below are the real, measured routes between them — including the broken ones.

The instructions

/guide

44 pages: FEMA's Tasks 1–8 (1–5 genuinely strong), an "Enter Forms Data" page listing the six forms, one detailed form how-to (Actions only), and a technical guide. It is the only surface that tells a planner the admin panel exists — and it does so in prose, because the entire Task flow contains zero working hyperlinks.

• 59 of its 122 links (48%) are broken or placeholders — including every navigation card on its own two hub pages, and the six form cards sitting under "Click below to visit the Admin Page" that carry no links at all
• Its admin walkthrough cites a hardcoded county dev URL (chemung2025.devmny.org/admin/); the "Data Management" how-tos that Tasks 3–4 defer to are five empty pages

Where the forms are

/admin

Shared across every county subdomain, scoped by 66 per-subdomain geoid filter sets. Login required; county groups get view-page only. Nav shows three entries — Home, Forms, PDF Export — in a developer's vocabulary (single_edit_simple, list_search_old).

• 112 pages; a planner's six forms are ~15 of them
• No status, no "what's left", no link back into the plan you came from

Scale honesty

Outside Actions (18k rows statewide), every county-entered dataset is pilot-county scale — Chemung and Sullivan mostly. The redesign isn't migrating a mature system; it's choosing the shape before 60 more counties arrive.

Env hygiene

All of this lives in a dmsEnv named test_meta_forms_env where 28 of 51 sources are name-obvious test junk (form1, adamtest1–3, five "Test Form copy (1)" rows…) — ~65% carry no live value once dead duplicates are counted.

Two kinds of "data"

The plan also reads ~30 external risk-library sources (fusion events, NRI, floodplains, boundaries) from hazmit_dama. Planners never touch those — and the UI never tells them which kind they're looking at.

The detail that explains everything · Section 04

The plan's doors into the admin panel are dataset rows — and most of them are dead

The guide promises: "Annotation cards take you directly to the correct form in the admin panel." Mechanically, those cards are Card components bound to LHMP_IA — the admin URL is a value in column shmp_component_name_location, rendered as an "Add Data" link. Ten rows carry admin URLs. Seven point at *_add_new pages that no longer exist anywhere in the 112-page pattern.

Worse: three rows were repaired (Actions, HoC, Roles now target real create pages) — but template Card sections cache row snapshots, so ~58 sections per county site still hold the old dead URLs. Fixing the data didn't fix the sites. That fragility is itself a finding: the platform's most important workflow hand-off is an unvalidated string column.

Section 05

The two-paradigms problem, from a planner's chair

A county planner is an emergency manager, not a database admin. The platform currently asks them to hold two unexplained mental models at once:

In the county site (learned, trusted)

  • • "This is my plan. I click the light-blue box, I write, I save. What I see is what readers get."
  • • Guidance sits inline, next to the thing it explains.
  • • A hazard chapter shows ~74 sections in edit mode — but only the ~11 Annotation slots respond the way they expect; the other 58% are pre-bound state data components they've learned to scroll past.

In the admin panel (foreign, unexplained)

  • • A different app: side-nav instead of the plan's chrome, tables instead of pages, no county identity, no way back.
  • • The nav offers "Forms" — then 14 Actions pages whose names (create_full, single_edit_simple) describe implementation history, not tasks.
  • • Nothing says why this table matters: that each row they add here will appear on their hazard chapters, their annexes, their dashboards.

The concept the platform never teaches

The real distinction isn't "pages vs admin" — it's "words you write in place" vs "records you enter once that appear everywhere." An action entered once surfaces on the hazard chapter, the annex, the actions database, and the state's rollups. That's the platform's superpower, and it is currently invisible: entry happens in a separate app, displays happen in the plan, and no UI ever draws the line between them. Whichever direction is chosen, the fix is the same — put the entry point next to the display it feeds ("Edit the data behind this table"), so the concept teaches itself at the moment it matters.

Section 06

The page nobody built: "How is my plan doing?"

Neither surface answers the planner's first question. The admin panel opens on charts of statewide totals; the plan opens on its public homepage. Nowhere is there a status overview: everything this plan must fill out, and whether it has been — the page that would orient a new planner, drive the work order, and give DHSES an honest progress signal. (Owner requirement, captured 2026-08-06: a status page, and possibly a status band on the landing page, in whatever the next admin becomes.)

The measured publish-lag makes this concrete: Westchester has 54 slots drafted and zero net published — to the public (and to any naive progress query) the county looks untouched. A real status page must read draft_sections, count form rows per jurisdiction, and say so.

Everything needed to drive it already exists

  • Annotation fill-state — 267–276 slots per county, filled/empty is one text-length test (measured for this report)
  • Form-row coverage per jurisdiction — the rules are already written in the guide: ≥1 action per hazard of concern per jurisdiction, ≥2 new/carryover actions, SFHA flood-action rule, ~16 HoC rows per jurisdiction
  • LHMP_IA's own columns — mny_required, complete, dhses_review, control_number (CN…) — a requirements checklist embedded in the template's config dataset, currently unused
  • DHSES_County_Database — the 62-county plan-status registry, the natural cross-county rollup

Section 07

Where this can go — two directions, three end-states

Each direction gets its own report. Four moves are no-regret and shared by every end-state: repair the 7 dead "Add Data" rows (+ refresh cached sections), fix the guide's hardcoded/malformed links, archive the "(old)"/Dup/Archived trees, and decide the Capabilities-vs-Capacities schema before more counties fill either.

A · Consolidate the panel B1 · Move planner work into the plan; keep a slim state console B2 · Retire the panel entirely
The planner's world still two apps, but the second one becomes coherent: ~30 role-adaptive pages, status landing, human names one app — every form entered inside the plan, next to the display it feeds; status on the plan itself same as B1
The state's world unchanged — QA and catalogs stay in the same panel planners use a renamed ~12-page console: cross-county status, catalog stewardship, QA queue, imports stewardship moves to the statewide site + generic datasets tooling — no dedicated console
Build size smallest — mockups + IA already exist (July report) largest for planner surfaces — but the actions flow is designed, its mechanics are live, and the template already hides half the workbench B1 + migration of state tooling
Risk polishes a context-switch planners already resist; the panel must still be taught role-gating inside county sites; template grows heavier; clone-drift must be solved for form pages loses the one place DHSES sees all counties at once — highest operational risk
Read the report Direction A → Direction B (B1 + B2) → recommended: B1 covered in Direction B →

Why B1 is the recommendation: the platform has already voted. The actions workflow moved into plan context and became the design precedent; the template grew its own form prototypes; the newest admin work (the Jurisdictional Entry copy) duplicates a template page rather than improving the panel. Direction A would make the panel a better version of a place planners were already routing around — worth doing only if build capacity rules out B1, and its status-landing work carries forward into B1 unchanged either way.
