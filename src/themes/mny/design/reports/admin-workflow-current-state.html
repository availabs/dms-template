The verdict

A cleaner panel — still standing outside the workflow.

Three counties are actively writing plans today — Suffolk (169 annotation slots drafted, 104 published), Schenectady (55), Westchester (54) — and that work happens inside their own county sites: filling the light-blue Annotation slots the template marks for them, reading the Inline Guidance beside each one, rendering jurisdictional annexes through a single page driven by ?geoid_juris= URL state. The template carries its own hidden data-entry workbench (Actions Edit List, Action View/Edit, form prototypes), and the /actions pattern runs the jurisdiction-picker → workspace flow in plan context.

The admin panel is 75 pages, and it is visibly being worked on: a single page-set per dataset, a new "Your Plan Data" landing that explains each dataset in plain language, a Forms hub carrying a draft redesign. What remains around that active core is the old pattern's residue: 21 pages serve datasets no county plan displays, four edit pages are bound to the wrong source, a third giant copy of the annex form lives here beside the template's two, and the only doors from the plan into the panel are dataset rows — half of which point at pages that don't exist, with dead URLs still cached into ~94 sections of every county site.

None of this means the panel's job disappeared. Six forms datasets are genuinely county-entered, required, and rendered throughout every plan. The question the two companion reports take up is where that job should live — in this panel, finished properly, or inside the plan the way actions already went.

The instructions

/guide

45 pages: FEMA's Tasks 1–8 (1–5 genuinely strong), an "Enter Forms Data" page listing the six forms, one detailed form how-to (Actions only), a technical guide — and two parallel versions of its own home page. It is the only surface that tells a planner the admin panel exists, and it does so entirely in prose: not one working link to /admin exists anywhere in the guide.

• 63 of its 131 published links are bare https:// placeholders — including every navigation card on both of its home pages
• the "Data Management" how-tos that Tasks 3–4 defer to are still empty pages; five of six forms still have no how-to

Where the forms are

/admin

Shared across every county subdomain, scoped by 66 per-subdomain geoid filter sets. Login required; county groups get view-page only. One page-set per dataset now, plus a plain-language "Your Plan Data" landing — with the hub's own redesign sitting unpublished in its draft sections.

• 75 pages; a planner's six forms are ~30 of them, and 21 more serve datasets no plan displays
• No status, no "what's left", and no link back into the plan you came from — the new landing included

Scale honesty

Outside Actions (18k rows statewide), every county-entered dataset is pilot-county scale — Chemung and Sullivan mostly. The redesign isn't migrating a mature system; it's choosing the shape before 60 more counties arrive.

Env hygiene

All of this lives in a dmsEnv named test_meta_forms_env where 28 of 51 sources are name-obvious test junk (form1, adamtest1–3, five "Test Form copy (1)" rows…) — ~65% carry no live value once dead duplicates are counted.

Two kinds of "data"

The plan also reads ~30 external risk-library sources (fusion events, NRI, floodplains, boundaries) from hazmit_dama. Planners never touch those — and outside the new landing's blurbs, the UI never tells them which kind they're looking at.

The detail that explains everything · Section 04

The plan's doors into the admin panel are dataset rows — and even repaired rows keep serving dead links

The guide promises: "Annotation cards take you directly to the correct form in the admin panel." Mechanically, those cards are Card components bound to LHMP_IA — the admin URL is a value in column shmp_component_name_location, rendered as an "Add Data" link. Ten rows carry admin URLs. Five resolve to real form hubs. Five point at *_add_new pages that exist nowhere in the 75-page pattern.

The sharper finding is what repair looks like: template Card sections cache row snapshots, so ~94 sections in every county site still render old dead URLs (hazards-of-concern ×34, capabilities ×34, actions ×22…) while ~56 sections show the new hub links — which one a visitor gets depends on each Card's fetch mode. Fixing the data does not fix the sites. The platform's most important workflow hand-off is an unvalidated string column plus a cache.

Section 05

The two-paradigms problem, from a planner's chair

A county planner is an emergency manager, not a database admin. The platform currently asks them to hold two unexplained mental models at once:

In the county site (learned, trusted)

  • • "This is my plan. I click the light-blue box, I write, I save. What I see is what readers get."
  • • Guidance sits inline, next to the thing it explains.
  • • A hazard chapter shows ~74 sections in edit mode — but only the ~11 Annotation slots respond the way they expect; the other 58% are pre-bound state data components they've learned to scroll past.

In the admin panel (foreign, unexplained)

  • • A different app: side-nav instead of the plan's chrome, tables instead of pages, no county identity, no way back.
  • • The forms still speak in implementation names — single_edit_new, edit_simple — and four of the six datasets no longer offer any page named for adding a record.
  • • Beyond the landing's blurbs, nothing says why a table matters: that each row added here appears on their hazard chapters, their annexes, their dashboards.

The concept the platform is starting to teach — in the wrong place

The real distinction isn't "pages vs admin" — it's "words you write in place" vs "records you enter once that appear everywhere." The panel's newest page finally writes this down, and well: "One row per jurisdiction per hazard… Your risk assessment chapter is written from this grid." But that sentence lives in the admin panel — the app planners visit least — while the tables it explains sit unlabeled across their plans. Whichever direction is chosen, the fix is the same: put the explanation and the entry point next to the display they feed, so the concept teaches itself at the moment it matters.

Section 06

The page nobody has built: "How is my plan doing?"

Neither surface answers the planner's first question. The plan opens on its public homepage; the admin opens on statewide totals, and its new "Your Plan Data" landing — the closest thing to an answer — describes each dataset and shows county figures without ever scoring them: no coverage against the guide's rules, no narrative fill, no "do this next." Nowhere is there a status overview: everything this plan must fill out, and whether it has been — the page that would orient a new planner, drive the work order, and give DHSES an honest progress signal. (Owner requirement, captured 2026-08-06; designed since as the Plan Status mockups, in both direction chromes.)

The measured publish-lag makes draft-awareness non-negotiable: Westchester has 54 slots drafted and 10 published — to the public (and to any naive progress query) the county looks barely started. A real status page must read draft_sections, count form rows per jurisdiction, and say so.

Everything needed to drive it already exists

  • Annotation fill-state — 267 slots per county, filled/empty is one text-length test (measured for this report)
  • Form-row coverage per jurisdiction — the rules are already written in the guide: ≥1 action per hazard of concern per jurisdiction, ≥2 new/carryover actions, SFHA flood-action rule, ~16 HoC rows per jurisdiction
  • FEMA review-element tags on the sections themselves — 1,866 template sections carry data.tags codes (B2-a, C4-b…), including 173 of the 256 narrative slots — the review-tool mapping Task 7 alludes to
  • LHMP_IA's own columns — mny_required (42 Required components), control_number — a requirements checklist in the template's config dataset, currently unused
  • DHSES_County_Database — the 62-county plan-status registry, the natural cross-county rollup

Section 07

Where this can go — two directions, three end-states

Each direction gets its own report. Four moves are no-regret and shared by every end-state: finish the "Add Data" repair (5 remaining rows and the cached section snapshots), give the guide working links and the five missing form how-tos, delete or re-bind the orphaned Other-Forms shells, and decide the Capabilities-vs-Capacities schema before more counties fill either.

A · Finish the panel B1 · Move planner work into the plan; keep a slim state console B2 · Retire the panel entirely
The planner's world still two apps, but the second one becomes coherent: task-named role-adaptive pages, a status landing, working doors — the current renovation is this direction's opening act one app — every form entered inside the plan, next to the display it feeds; status on the plan itself same as B1
The state's world unchanged — QA and catalogs stay in the same panel planners use a renamed ~12-page console: cross-county status, catalog stewardship, QA queue, imports stewardship moves to the statewide site + generic datasets tooling — no dedicated console
Build size smallest — the deletion half is largely done; what's left is the status landing, naming, and seam repairs largest for planner surfaces — but the actions flow is designed, its mechanics are live, and the template already hides half the workbench B1 + migration of state tooling
Risk polishes a context-switch planners already resist; the panel must still be taught role-gating inside county sites; template grows heavier; form surfaces must be shared patterns, not clones loses the one place DHSES sees all counties at once — highest operational risk
Read the report Direction A → Direction B (B1 + B2) → recommended: B1 covered in Direction B →

Why B1 is the recommendation: the platform keeps voting with its feet. The actions workflow moved into plan context and became the design precedent; the template grew its own form prototypes; the counties do their real work in-site and Suffolk just published a hundred sections of it. A leaner, better-explained panel is genuinely better — and it is still a second app on the far side of a broken bridge. The renovation's deletions and its plain-language landing carry forward into B1 unchanged; the direction reports say where the rest goes.
