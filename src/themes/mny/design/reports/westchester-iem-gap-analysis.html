The headline

The content is there.
Nobody can see it.

Pattern 2448336 holds 256 Local Context (Annotation) boxes. In draft_sections, 185 of them have text. In the published sections that every visitor actually sees, 8 do — and those 8 are county-template pre-fills that were already there.

So 177 sections, 334,761 characters of IEM’s base plan, were loaded correctly on 8 September and have been invisible ever since. Draft and published are separate component rows in DMS; writing to the draft changes nothing on screen until the page is published. No page in this pattern has ever been published.

This is one action, not 177: publish the 30 affected pages. It accounts for the large majority of what IEM reported.

Seen live, 10 Sep 2026

/the_local_environment/people_and_communities — the LOCAL CONTEXT box renders as an empty grey rectangle. Draft section 2454034 behind it holds 4,402 characters of Westchester demographic narrative. The Demographic Statistics card beside it correctly shows 996.9K population, so the geoid is right and the data layer is working.

Also worth knowing

The subdomain changed. Every prior document points at westchester-2026.mitigateny.org, which now returns 404. The pattern’s subdomain is westchester, so the live site is westchester.mitigateny.org. Worth confirming with IEM that they were looking at the right one.

Worth putting to IEM plainly

Local Capabilities and Local Actions are the narrative boxes that sit directly above the capabilities and actions tables. IEM may have left them blank on purpose, expecting the tables below to carry the content. That is a reasonable reading of the 2.0 architecture — and it makes the table problems in sections 4–6 the real issue, not the empty boxes.

The Home page still reads as a template

Live text on /home: “This is a county template for local hazard mitigation plans. Neither replaced the template copy.

Hazards of Concern · internal_source/1473470/version/1473471

The table works. Four of its columns are blank in the workbook.

The jurisdictional Hazards of Concern table draws correctly: 45 rows, one per jurisdiction, on every hazard page — verified live on Flooding, Hail and Earthquake. The rows are there. What is missing is what is inside them.

The workbook’s own header for General Vulnerability & Impact reads “get from community insights summary” followed by a SharePoint link — that content was never pulled into the file. This is a source gap, not a migration gap: nothing was lost in transfer.

Separately, the small Hazards-of-Concern summary card at the top of every hazard page returns nothing, and always will. It filters geoid_juris with the county geoid, and geoid_juris only ever holds jurisdiction geoids. Its one value column is general_vulnerability, which is blank anyway, so fixing the filter alone will not make it render.

A latent trap, not currently biting

Every hazard page carries a hazards page variable still set to the retired lower-case codes — riverine on Flooding, winterweat on Snowstorm, coldwave on Extreme Cold, heatwave on Extreme Heat. The Hazards of Concern data uses the current title-case names. Today the components ignore the page variable and use their own correct seed, which is why the tables show 45 rows. The moment anything starts honouring that variable, every one of these tables drops from 45 rows to about 5. Also note the data stores Ice storm with a lower-case s while the page variable says Ice Storm.

Actions · internal_source/1029065/version/1074456

1,095 actions loaded. The site thinks almost all of them are flood actions.

The actions themselves came through well — 1,095 Westchester rows, with problem statements on 1,089, solutions on all 1,095, action type on 1,087 and priority on 1,080.

What did not come through is the hazard tagging. IEM’s workbook marks each action against individual hazards in its own per-hazard columns — Wind on 175 actions, Snowstorm on 135, Hurricane on 128, Coastal Hazards on 112, and so on. In the live source those columns are all but empty, and hazard association survives only in primary_hazard_type, which is 776 Flooding out of 1,095.

The hazard pages filter on primary / secondary / tertiary hazard type only. So the Local Actions card and table are empty on Avalanche, Coastal Hazards, Drought, Hail, Ice Storm and Tornado, and thin everywhere except Flooding. Confirmed live: Hail 0 rows, Earthquake 0 rows, Flooding 355 rows.

The Actions Dashboard tells the same story in one screen: it reports 545 actions across seven hazards — Flooding 356, Most/all 120, Snowstorm 34, Extreme Heat 6, Wildfire 5, Earthquake 1, Hurricane 1. Nine hazards the workbook tags are absent entirely.

What to do, in order

Four of ours, three of theirs

On our side

  1. Publish the pattern. 30 pages, 177 sections, 334,761 characters. This alone answers most of what IEM reported. Everything is read-back verified and nothing else changes.
  2. Drop the geoid_juris clause from the four Local Capabilities Tables (2469050, 2463313, 2450074, 2463317). That is 43 rows → up to 2,039.
  3. Fix the Actions hazard rollup, either by reloading the Actions sheet or by giving the Actions filters a calculated hazards branch. Until then the site presents Westchester as a flood-only county.
  4. Work the punch list in section 7 — four one-line filter corrections, plus the two capability-type spellings, which are worth fixing statewide.

Sequencing note. Publishing first is safe and reversible per page, and it changes what IEM sees more than the other three combined. Do it before the next review call.

To send back to IEM

Only People and Communities was filled. The header points at a SharePoint community-insights summary that never made it into the file.
  2. Local Capabilities, Local Actions and Featured Strategy have no prose under any of their 48 headings in the base plan, and the whole Avalanche profile is empty. Confirm whether that is deliberate — if the intent is that the tables carry it, say so and we will hide the boxes rather than leave them blank.
  3. Mount Pleasant has no annex, and the Jurisdictional Profile and Growth and Development Trends boxes are empty on all 45 annexes. The second is a 44 CFR element.
  4. The County Executive’s transmittal letter is still a placeholder in the document, so the Home page carries template copy.
  5. Minor: the base plan gives two different open-space inventories (~70,000 ac in table 760 vs 82,490.5 ac in table 768, different category schemes). Which should the site show?

About anchor_url

The CSV gives you <page_url>#<draft_section_id> as requested, but it will not jump yet. A DMS section only renders a DOM id when its author sets anchorId (or navLabel) in the section settings, and none of the 1,802 components in this pattern has either (section.jsx:273).

One CLI pass can set anchorId = the section’s own id on all 256 Annotation slots. It is draft-only, invisible in view mode, and makes every anchor_url in this CSV work. Say the word and I will run it. Until then, use edit_url and find the section by its title and section_order.
