The verdict

The template renders.
It just keeps telling you
the wrong thing confidently.

This is a walkthrough, not a config audit. Twenty pages were opened as a first-time reader would open them, and every observation below was read off the rendered page — text content, computed styles, measured element positions, navigation timing — then traced back to what produces it.

Nothing here is a crash or a blank page. Every map draws, every tile route is healthy, the navigation works. The problem is subtler and worse: a hazard mitigation plan’s entire value is that the numbers are right for this county and this hazard, and seven separate things on these pages tell the reader they might not be — a table of drought measures on the extreme-cold page, twelve hazard pages pointing at “the flooding profile,” a raw GEOID where a town name belongs, and half a second of the previous hazard’s risk rating after you’ve already clicked away.

The other ten findings are cheaper but compound the same impression. Headings with nothing under them are legitimate — this is a template and county authors fill those in — but nothing on screen says so, so an unfilled slot and a broken one look identical. And a card group drawn to read as one box has a 32 px gap at every junction and no bottom edge on fifteen of sixteen hazard pages.

Tier 1

The reader starts doubting the data

Seven things that read as “this is showing me the wrong information.” Each one tells the reader, in a different way, that the plan’s figures may not be theirs.

01

Clicking from one hazard to another shows you the previous hazard

reproduced
click url /…/wind card WIND · HIGH RISK t = 1400ms url /…/drought card WIND · HIGH RISK ← address says drought, page says wind t = 1800ms url /…/drought card DROUGHT · MODERATE RISK
Where
Every in-app navigation between hazard profiles. Not reproducible on a hard refresh.
Why
Each section stores the rows it last fetched (element-data.data) and paints them immediately while the new request is in flight. On a fresh load there is no stale cache to paint; on an in-app hop the previous hazard’s rows are still mounted.
How far
All 16 hazard pages, and any page pair sharing a component template. The most damaging item in this report — a reader who catches it once has to re-verify everything else.
02

Twelve hazard pages tell you to go read “the flooding profile”

12 of 16
Detailed descriptions, characteristics, and the extent of extreme cold that impacts New York State communities, including this county, are included in the flooding profile of the MitigateNY State Hazard Mitigation Plan (SHMP)…
Where
Earthquake, Wind, Snowstorm, Wildfire, Extreme Cold, Extreme Heat, Hail, Lightning, Hurricane, Coastal Hazards, Ice Storm, Landslide. Only Flooding reads correctly — it is the page the sentence was written for.
Why
The narrative is not authored per page — it is a row in LHMP_IA selected by a hazard filter. The first half of each sentence was localised; the trailing clause was not. One data-entry error, replicated by the template.
How far
Twelve pages, fixed in twelve dataset rows — not twelve components.
03

“Measures Inventory” lists all 249 measures on every hazard page

13 pages
MEASURES INVENTORY — on the Extreme Cold page Building Codes · Bridge Strengthening · Alternative Routes and Redundancy · Acquisition Arson Prevention · Abatement Districts · Building Construction · Backup Power Systems Assess Vulnerability to Drought Risk · 9-1-1 and 3-1-1 PAGE 1 OF 25 Rows 1 to 10 of 249
Where
13 of the 16 hazard pages — 249 rows on nine, 85 on four, 24 on landslide. Those differing counts are themselves stale caches (finding 12).
Why
The section carries no filter of any kind — no stored predicate, no column filter — while its page publishes geoid, hazards, hazards2 and hazards3. The pager is honest; the binding is not. Turning pagination off would hide the bug rather than fix it.
How far
Same shape as “Local Actions Table” (196 rows) on the hazard pages, plus three more elsewhere: built_environment (254), natural_environment “Potential Impacts Table” (19), nfip_floodplain_management “Floodplain Administrators” (11).
04

The jurisdiction picker shows a raw ten-digit code instead of a place name

visible
Select the jurisdictional annex: Jurisdiction 3610506310 BETHEL (TOWN)
Where
/the_plan/jurisdictional_annexes/select_jurisdiction — the first interactive element on the page.
Why
The live Jurisdictions source defines a label lookup for geoid (view 1346450, geoid → municipality_name). The component’s stored copy of that column has no lookup at all, so it renders the stored value — the code.
How far
Two components render it visibly; 54 more carry the same column hidden, so it surfaces the moment anyone toggles it on.
05

One hazard in the list is spelled differently from all the others

visible
Wind · Extreme Cold · Snowstorm · Ice storm · Drought · Flooding · Hail · Landslide · Wildfire
Where
The “Hazards of Concern by Jurisdiction” table on The Risk → Natural Hazards, and anywhere else that column renders.
Why
The source’s label map is correct and complete — it maps icestorm → “Ice Storm”, and all 19 entries match the component’s stored copy exactly. So “Ice storm” is not coming from the lookup; it is a stored row value that isn’t one of the 19 keys, so it falls through and renders raw.
How far
One value confirmed — but the mechanism affects every lookup-backed column, and it is precisely why a configuration-only audit reports this column clean.
06

A count of disasters is rendered as “0.0”

home page
# OF DECLARED DISASTERS 13 2 2 0.0 # OF EVENTS 13 85 57 103
Where
The four hazard cards on the home page, read across — and on Extreme Cold, directly above “# OF NON-DECLARED DISASTERS 11”.
Why
A count column with no number format applied. Where the value is a whole number the underlying type never shows; where it is zero, the raw float does.
How far
Every hazard whose declared-disaster count is zero. On the home page that is one of four cards above the fold.
07

Internal system vocabulary is used as user-facing labels

16+ pages
MEASURES INVENTORY Category-Deprecated [select…] Type [select…] HAZARDS OF CONCERN BY JURISDICTION Jurisdiction GeoID [select…] Hazard [select…] Hazard of Concern [Yes] DEC DAM INVENTORY Hazard Text [High Hazard Dam] Municipalities [select…]
Where
Category-Deprecated on all 16 hazard pages; Jurisdiction GeoID on The Risk → Natural Hazards; Hazard Text on High Hazard Dams.
Why
The facet label is the source column’s title, and the data steward has been using that title as a workspace — “Category-Deprecated” is an instruction to other data people that ended up on a public filter. The component’s own stored copy still reads “Category”, which is why nobody editing the page would notice: the marker exists only on the live source. Every option inside Jurisdiction GeoID is a place name, so the label is also simply wrong for its own contents.
How far
32 components across 16 pages render the deprecated-titled column. A further 30 columns on Capabilities_Catalogue are titled “(Delete) …” and sit one show toggle away from the same outcome.
Tier 2

The plan looks unfinished

Some of this is legitimate — it is a template, and the empty slots wait on county authors. The problem is that nothing on screen says so, so a reader can’t tell an intentional blank from a broken one.

These are rich-text sections with a title and an empty body — deliberate placeholders. That is the right design; the presentation is the problem. There is no empty state: no “awaiting local input” marker, no prompt shown in the slot. 10

The same heading eight times on one page

26 instances

Scrolling Built Environment, you pass a section titled LOCAL CONTEXT — then another, then another, eight times, each empty, with no indication of what each one is the local context for.

built_environment ×8 capabilities_assessment ×4 natural_environment ×4 people_and_communities ×3 strategies ×3 about_the_process ×2 disasters ×2

The title is generic by design — one “Local Context” slot per preceding data block. But the page renders them as peers in a flat scroll rather than as children of the block they belong to, so the relationship that makes the title meaningful is invisible. A county author filling them in hits the same problem in the editor’s section list.

11

Four different tables on one page, all titled “State Mitigation Capabilities”

STATE MITIGATION CAPABILITIES Rows 1 to 10 of 41 (+ Local Context, empty) STATE MITIGATION CAPABILITIES Rows 1 to 10 of 243 (+ Local Context, empty) STATE MITIGATION CAPABILITIES Rows 1 to 10 of 11 (+ Local Context, empty) STATE MITIGATION CAPABILITIES Rows 1 to 10 of 148 (+ Local Context, empty)

Each is scoped to a different capability category, but the distinguishing information lives only in the filter applied — not in the title. A reader has no way to tell what separates the 41-row table from the 243-row one. And per finding 12, all four are bound to the same source and view, so the four counts cannot all be current. This is the page a FEMA reviewer opens to check capability documentation.

12

Row counts that contradict each other for the same table

systemic
Measures Inventory — identical unfiltered binding, sixteen pages nine pages → 249 four pages → 85 landslide → 24 capabilities_assessment — twelve unfiltered sections, one source, one view totals reported: 0, 1, 2, 3, 11, 41, 148, 243

Each section stores the row count from the last time it was saved and renders that number in the pager before the live count arrives. Two sections with identical bindings cannot honestly disagree, so where they do, at least one is stale. Same cached-state mechanism as finding 1, surfacing as a number rather than as content. The practical consequence: no pager total on this site can be trusted as reported — which also means the empty tables in finding 8 need re-checking against live data before any of them is called a data bug.

Tier 3

The product looks unpolished

The “something’s off about this page” findings. Individually small; collectively they are what makes a site read as unmaintained.

13

The card group meant to read as one box has 32px gaps and no bottom edge

measured
Measured
Extreme Cold, sections 2384117 → 2384118 → 2384119 → 2384120. Inner box edges at y = 942–1060, 1092–1214, 1246–1430, 1462–1849 — gaps of exactly 32px at every junction. The final box’s bottom border is rgba(0,0,0,0) with border-radius: 0.
Why
Two causes stacked. (a) Section padding is an outer gutter — the border draws on a box inside it — so p-4 on each member inserts 16px above and below, pushing the boxes 32px apart. That is the opposite of the intuitive reading, which is why it survives review. (b) The run goes openBottom → borderX → borderX → borderX and never gets its closing openTop.
How far
15 of the 16 hazard pages. Flooding is the exception — its run ends on the closer and does draw a bottom edge, so that one page is the specification for fixing the other fifteen. Separately the 32px gap affects every fused group site-wide, including the ones that do close, so closing the runs alone will not make the borders touch.

The paddings are hand-nudged spacers — someone aligning two columns by eye at one viewport width. Those offsets don’t track content, so they only hold at the width they were tuned at, and the 1px one is doing nothing except breaking the alignment it was meant to fix. Three pairs per hazard page. This is what the platform’s grid alignment settings exist to handle; the manual padding works against them.

15

Numbers in one row of cards are aligned three different ways

16 pages
block 1 (unset) block 2 left block 3 (unset) block 4 center

Inside the same four-part risk card. Nothing looks wrong on its own; the row looks wrong. Three treatments because three edits happened at three different times, and the platform has three distinct “no value” states — unset, empty string, and a single space — that all look identical in the editor. Pattern-wide, left is the dominant explicit choice at 69% of set values, so left is the existing convention. Choosing center for this group is a legitimate design decision, not a defect — the defensible finding is the three-way split, not which value wins. 16 of 82 fused groups site-wide disagree internally, and all 16 are this template.

16

The same table has different column headings on different pages

Other Hazard Events — the same data, two pages on a hazard page FUSION YEAR PRIMARY HAZARD CATEGORY TOTAL DAMAGE (WITH POPULATION) on Natural Hazards YEAR NRI CATEGORY TOTAL DAMAGE

And on Natural Hazards one table carries two adjacent headings differing only by a parenthetical: DISASTER NUMBER then DISASTER NUMBER (# ONLY). Column display names are overridden per component, so two copies of one table drift apart as each is edited. A reader comparing the county roll-up against a hazard profile cannot tell whether “Total Damage” and “Total Damage (with population)” are the same measure — which, for a damage figure in a mitigation plan, is a substantive question, not a cosmetic one.

17

Facet dropdowns with nothing in them

minor

On Track Progress → Funding Sources, five select… controls sit in a filter bar and three offer no options at all. On High Hazard Dams, a “Hazard Level” column reads High Hazard Dam on all eleven rows — a column of one repeated value, on a page called High Hazard Dams. It is technically correct and informationally empty: the page’s own filter, restated once per row.

Clean

Checked, and not a problem

Recorded so nobody spends time on them — and because three of these are cases where the stored configuration looks wrong and the rendered page is fine.

Every map renders

Canvases confirmed on Extreme Cold (2), Natural Hazards, NFIP Floodplain and High Hazard Dams. All 196 map layers use the live tile route, all match the view_{id} convention, and 60 live ?cols= probes found no layer requesting a column its view lacks.

The “Modeled RIsk” typo

The stored title on 16 sections has a capital I — but the heading is CSS-uppercased, so it renders MODELED RISK and no visitor can see it. Editor-facing only; reporting it as a front-end defect would have been wrong.

White buttons on the beige band

Configuration shows 62 cells whose colour differs from their section. On climate_change and strategies the beige cards paint as one surface and no visible mismatch reproduced. Latent risk, not a current defect.

The hazard label map

Components’ stored copy of the 19-entry map is identical in content to the live source’s. The “metadata out of date” flag on this column is a false positive — which is exactly why finding 5 needed a live check to surface.

Overview text on the Wind page

Its stored filter reads hazards = ["Flooding"], which looks serious. At runtime the page variable overrides it and the correct wind text renders. Stale stored config, no visitor impact.

Navigation, breadcrumbs, footer

Consistent across all 20 pages walked. Breadcrumb, top nav and footer link sets all resolve.

Recommended path

Seventeen findings, nine jobs — and the first one buys the most

1 · Loading state, not stale content

Stop painting the previous fetch’s rows while a request is in flight, and stop rendering the cached row count in the pager. Findings 1 and 12. One platform change; removes the worst item and makes every pager total honest.

2 · Scope the unfiltered tables

Bind Measures Inventory and Local Actions Table to the page’s hazard variable. Finding 3. 13 pages × 2 components — one template edit propagated.

3 · Fix the dataset rows

Twelve LHMP_IA narrative rows ending in “the flooding profile”; one Ice storm value that isn’t a valid key. Findings 2 and 5. Data entry — no component change fixes either.

4 · Add the missing lookups

Two visible columns rendering raw GEOIDs. Finding 4. Refresh those named columns — not a blanket refresh, which overwrites author overrides.

5 · Rename what the reader sees

Category-Deprecated → a real label (and retire the column); Jurisdiction GeoID → “Jurisdiction”; Hazard Text → “Hazard Level”. Reconcile the two Other Hazard Events header sets. Findings 7, 16, 17.

6 · Close and fuse the card runs

Add the closing preset, move the 16px from section padding to the card’s inner padding, drop the 1/50/100px spacers, settle on one alignment. Findings 13, 14, 15. Fix on flooding’s pattern and re-propagate to 15 pages.

7 · Give empty things an empty state

A zero-row table should say so instead of rendering “Page 1 of 0”; an unfilled placeholder should show its authoring prompt in the slot instead of hiding it behind an icon. Findings 8, 9, 17.

8 · Number formatting

Apply an integer format to the declared-disaster counts. Finding 6.

9 · Editorial

Disambiguate the four “State Mitigation Capabilities” titles and the repeated “Local Context” headings — or nest them under the block they describe so the generic title reads correctly. Findings 10, 11.

Net effect: jobs 1–4 are the ones a reader would notice within thirty seconds of arriving, and between them they touch one platform behaviour, one template binding, thirteen dataset rows and two column definitions. Everything in Tier 3 is real but can wait — a reader forgives a 32px seam much more readily than a drought measure on the extreme-cold page.

Method & limits

20 of 55 pages walked — home, four section landings, six hazard profiles (extreme cold, wind, drought, avalanche, flooding + spot checks), built environment, people and communities, natural environment, NFIP floodplain, high hazard dams, climate change, disasters, about the process, strategies, capabilities assessment, select jurisdiction, actions database, funding sources. Counts stated as “16 hazard pages” or “site-wide” come from stored configuration and are labelled as such.

Read at 1440×1200, light theme, logged out. No mobile or narrow-viewport pass — given finding 14’s hand-tuned pixel paddings, a responsive check is likely to surface more and is worth doing separately.

No screenshots. The browser pane wouldn’t composite frames, so everything visual here is measured rather than seen — computed styles, element bounding boxes, rendered text. More precise for gaps and alignment; unable to catch colour contrast or a mis-cropped image, which are therefore not covered.

Not covered: accessibility (contrast, focus order, screen-reader labelling), print/PDF export, performance beyond the 1.8s navigation timing, authenticated and edit-mode views, and the 35 pages not walked. Sullivan County only — findings 2, 5 and 8 depend partly on which rows exist for this county.
