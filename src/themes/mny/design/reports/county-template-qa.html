The verdict

The template renders.
It just keeps telling you
the wrong thing confidently.

This is a walkthrough, not a config audit. Twenty pages were opened as a first-time reader would open them, and every observation below was read off the rendered page — text content, computed styles, measured element positions, navigation timing — then traced back to what produces it.

Nothing here is a crash or a blank page. Every map draws, every tile route is healthy, the navigation works. The problem is subtler and worse: a hazard mitigation plan’s entire value is that the numbers are right for this county and this hazard, and seven separate things on these pages tell the reader they might not be — a table of drought measures on the extreme-cold page, twelve hazard pages pointing at “the flooding profile,” a raw GEOID where a town name belongs, and half a second of the previous hazard’s risk rating after you’ve already clicked away.

The other nine findings are cheaper but compound the same impression: eleven illustrations that render as their own filename, a pager that counts to zero, and a card group drawn to read as one box that has a 32 px gap at every junction and no bottom edge on fifteen of sixteen hazard pages. Unfilled content slots are deliberately not counted — an empty “Local Context” awaiting county input is a template doing its job.

Behind the walkthrough sits a configuration sweep, reported separately as a to-do list of 1,274 items in six types. Most of it is maintenance a reader never sees, but one type is not: 82 of the 372 components carrying DHSES-authored SHMP narrative have no permission lock, so a county author can rewrite state-supplied text. That has no visible symptom at all, which is exactly why a walkthrough alone would have missed it.

Tier 1

The reader starts doubting the data

Seven things that read as “this is showing me the wrong information.” Each one tells the reader, in a different way, that the plan’s figures may not be theirs.

01

Clicking from one hazard to another shows you the previous hazard

reproduced
click url /…/wind card WIND · HIGH RISK t = 1400ms url /…/drought card WIND · HIGH RISK ← address says drought, page says wind t = 1800ms url /…/drought card DROUGHT · MODERATE RISK
Where
Every in-app navigation between hazard profiles. Not reproducible on a hard refresh.
Why
Each section stores the rows it last fetched (element-data.data) and paints them immediately while the new request is in flight. On a fresh load there is no stale cache to paint; on an in-app hop the previous hazard’s rows are still mounted.
How far
All 16 hazard pages, and any page pair sharing a component template. The most damaging item in this report — a reader who catches it once has to re-verify everything else.
02

Twelve hazard pages tell you to go read “the flooding profile”

12 of 16
Detailed descriptions, characteristics, and the extent of extreme cold that impacts New York State communities, including this county, are included in the flooding profile of the MitigateNY State Hazard Mitigation Plan (SHMP)…
Where
Earthquake, Wind, Snowstorm, Wildfire, Extreme Cold, Extreme Heat, Hail, Lightning, Hurricane, Coastal Hazards, Ice Storm, Landslide. Only Flooding reads correctly — it is the page the sentence was written for.
Why
The narrative is not authored per page — it is a row in LHMP_IA selected by a hazard filter. The first half of each sentence was localised; the trailing clause was not. One data-entry error, replicated by the template.
How far
Twelve pages, fixed in twelve dataset rows — not twelve components.

visible

Where
/the_plan/jurisdictional_annexes/select_jurisdiction — the first interactive element on the page.

visible

The “Hazards of Concern by Jurisdiction” table on The Risk → Natural Hazards, and anywhere else that column renders.

16+ pages

Where
Category-Deprecated on all 16 hazard pages; Jurisdiction GeoID on The Risk → Natural Hazards; Hazard Text on High Hazard Dams.

Each is scoped to a different capability category, but the distinguishing information lives only in the filter applied — not in the title. 11

Row counts that contradict each other for the same table

systemic
Measures Inventory — identical unfiltered binding, sixteen pages nine pages → 249 four pages → 85 landslide → 24 capabilities_assessment — twelve unfiltered sections, one source, one view totals reported: 0, 1, 2, 3, 11, 41, 148, 243

Each section stores the row count from the last time it was saved and renders that number in the pager before the live count arrives. Two sections with identical bindings cannot honestly disagree, so where they do, at least one is stale. Same cached-state mechanism as finding 1, surfacing as a number rather than as content. The practical consequence: no pager total on this site can be trusted as reported — which also means the empty tables in finding 9 need re-checking against live data before any of them is called a data bug.

Tier 3

The product looks unpolished

Five “something’s off about this page” findings. Individually small; collectively they are what makes a site read as unmaintained.

12

The card group meant to read as one box has 32px gaps and no bottom edge

measured
Measured
Extreme Cold, sections 2384117 → 2384118 → 2384119 → 2384120. Inner box edges at y = 942–1060, 1092–1214, 1246–1430, 1462–1849 — gaps of exactly 32px at every junction. The final box’s bottom border is rgba(0,0,0,0) with border-radius: 0.
Why
Two causes stacked. (a) Section padding is an outer gutter — the border draws on a box inside it — so p-4 on each member inserts 16px above and below, pushing the boxes 32px apart. That is the opposite of the intuitive reading, which is why it survives review. (b) The run goes openBottom → borderX → borderX → borderX and never gets its closing openTop.
How far
15 of the 16 hazard pages. Flooding is the exception — its run ends on the closer and does draw a bottom edge, so that one page is the specification for fixing the other fifteen. Separately the 32px gap affects every fused group site-wide, including the ones that do close, so closing the runs alone will not make the borders touch.

The paddings are hand-nudged spacers — someone aligning two columns by eye at one viewport width. Those offsets don’t track content, so they only hold at the width they were tuned at, and the 1px one is doing nothing except breaking the alignment it was meant to fix. Three pairs per hazard page. 14

Numbers in one row of cards are aligned three different ways

16 pages
block 1 (unset) block 2 left block 3 (unset) block 4 center

Inside the same four-part risk card. Nothing looks wrong on its own; the row looks wrong. Three treatments because three edits happened at three different times, and the platform has three distinct “no value” states — unset, empty string, and a single space — that all look identical in the editor. Pattern-wide, left is the dominant explicit choice at 69% of set values, so left is the existing convention. Choosing center for this group is a legitimate design decision, not a defect — the defensible finding is the three-way split, not which value wins. 16 of 82 fused groups site-wide disagree internally, and all 16 are this template.

15

The same table has different column headings on different pages

Other Hazard Events — the same data, two pages on a hazard page FUSION YEAR PRIMARY HAZARD CATEGORY TOTAL DAMAGE (WITH POPULATION) on Natural Hazards YEAR NRI CATEGORY TOTAL DAMAGE

And on Natural Hazards one table carries two adjacent headings differing only by a parenthetical: DISASTER NUMBER then DISASTER NUMBER (# ONLY). Column display names are overridden per component, so two copies of one table drift apart as each is edited. A reader comparing the county roll-up against a hazard profile cannot tell whether “Total Damage” and “Total Damage (with population)” are the same measure — which, for a damage figure in a mitigation plan, is a substantive question, not a cosmetic one.

minor

It is technically correct and informationally empty: the page’s own filter, restated once per row.

Recommended path

Sixteen findings, 1,274 items — ten jobs, and the first four buy the most

1 · Loading state, not stale content

Stop painting the previous fetch’s rows while a request is in flight, and stop rendering the cached row count in the pager. Findings 1 and 11. One platform change; removes the worst item and makes every pager total honest.

2 · Lock the SHMP narrative

Apply the LHMP Template Editor group to the 82 unlocked LHMP_IA components, and decide what the 9 lock-nobody and 3 odd-grant rows should be. To-do type 1. The only item here where the downside is a county overwriting state-authored text.

3 · Scope the unfiltered tables

Bind Measures Inventory and Local Actions Table to the page’s hazard variable. Finding 3. 13 pages × 2 components — one template edit propagated.

4 · Restore or drop the 11 images

Re-upload the ten missing bucket files or repoint those sections at the theme illustration set; replace the Audubon hotlink with a hosted copy. Finding 8, to-do type 2.

5 · Fix the dataset rows

Twelve LHMP_IA narrative rows ending in “the flooding profile”; one Ice storm value that isn’t a valid key. Findings 2 and 5. Data entry — no component change fixes either.

6 · Add the missing lookups, refresh the drifted columns

Two visible columns rendering raw GEOIDs, plus the 285 real metadata drifts — dominated by two source columns that gained new option values (other, Not Reported). Finding 4, to-do type 4. Refresh the named columns, not everything — refresh overwrites author overrides.

7 · Rename what the reader sees

Category-Deprecated → a real label (and retire the column); Jurisdiction GeoID → “Jurisdiction”; Hazard Text → “Hazard Level”. Reconcile the two Other Hazard Events header sets. Findings 7, 15, 16.

8 · Close and fuse the card runs

Add the closing preset, move the 16px from section padding to the card’s inner padding, drop the 1/50/100px spacers, settle on one alignment. Findings 12, 13, 14. Fix on flooding’s pattern and re-propagate to 15 pages.

9 · Empty states, and the tables that have none

A zero-row table should say so instead of rendering “Page 1 of 0”; the 20 zero-row sections need splitting into genuinely-empty (wants an empty state) and broken-binding (wants a fix), starting with the two Jurisdictions headers that pin no view. Findings 9, 16; to-do type 3.

10 · The bulk governance pass

875 untagged content sections, and a decision on fetch mode now that it is null on all 609 data components. To-do types 5 and 6. Neither is visible to a reader; both block the plan-element coverage view the tags exist to produce.

Net effect: jobs 1–5 are the ones a reader would notice within thirty seconds of arriving, and between them they touch one platform behaviour, one template binding, eleven image files, thirteen dataset rows and two column definitions. Job 2 is the exception to that rule and still ranks second: nobody sees a missing permission lock, but it is the only item whose failure mode is state-authored plan text being silently rewritten. Everything in Tier 3 is real and can wait — a reader forgives a 32px seam much more readily than a drought measure on the extreme-cold page.

Method & limits

20 of 55 pages walked — home, four section landings, six hazard profiles (extreme cold, wind, drought, avalanche, flooding + spot checks), built environment, people and communities, natural environment, NFIP floodplain, high hazard dams, climate change, disasters, about the process, strategies, capabilities assessment, select jurisdiction, actions database, funding sources. Counts stated as “16 hazard pages” or “site-wide” come from stored configuration and are labelled as such.

Read at 1440×1200, light theme, logged out. No mobile or narrow-viewport pass — given finding 13’s hand-tuned pixel paddings, a responsive check is likely to surface more and is worth doing separately.

No screenshots. The browser pane wouldn’t composite frames, so everything visual here is measured rather than seen — computed styles, element bounding boxes, rendered text. More precise for gaps and alignment; unable to catch colour contrast or a mis-cropped image, which are therefore not covered.

Not covered: accessibility (contrast, focus order, screen-reader labelling), print/PDF export, performance beyond the 1.8s navigation timing, authenticated and edit-mode views, and the 35 pages not walked.
